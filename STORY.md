# Labyrinth of Roots: Game Master Script

**Created by: Adrian Blackshire and Claude**

The full adventure as written for the game master. Text in *italics* inside parentheses is a mechanical note or a branch for the GM.

## Part 1: The Pathway

For two days you have walked the ceremonial pathway, pausing only to eat a few bites from your pack. The labyrinth waits somewhere below. On the road, two hooded strangers step into your path. "Turn back," one says. "Nothing that goes down there comes back up." You smile, press three coins into their palms, and walk on.

You descend level after level until the outer gate of the labyrinth rises before you. Its stone face is covered in druidic carvings: spirals, antlered figures, and runes that seem to shift when you look away. A chill settles in your gut. Perhaps the strangers knew something after all.

The sun sinks, washing the stone in violet, and then the light is gone. Darkness, total and absolute.

*Roll Perception.* (On a failure:) You never hear it coming. Behind you, something unfolds to its full height. It's a vine horror, nearly eight feet tall, and its thick, thorned limbs are made for tearing flesh. One lashes past your ear. *Roll initiative, or run.*

You flee into the corridors and turn blindly left, then right, then left again, until the rustling behind you fades. You've lost it, for now. In the pitch black, you need a cloth, a stick, and your flint. *Make a Survival check.* (On a success:) Sparks catch, and the torch flares to life.

Then you see where you are. Thick white webbing fills the corridor from floor to ceiling. Skeletons hang in the strands like flies, dozens of them, some still in rusted armor and clutching their weapons.

## Part 2: The Webs

The smell of death and decay fills your nose and throat. A warm wind brushes your skin, though there should be no wind down here. Then something hairy and jointed reaches out of the dark toward you.

Your torch sputters. Your heart sinks. *Make a Dexterity check.* (On a success:) You drop into a crouch, smother the flame, and spot a gap beneath the webbing where the strands are loose enough to crawl through. You slip under them on your belly, silk brushing your neck, and keep crawling until the passage opens.

## Part 3: The Hollow Chamber

You emerge into a round room that feels less built than grown. The walls curve up like the inside of a great hollowed tree, ridged with bark and knotted roots that twist into the ceiling. Thorny bushes sprout from cracks in the floor, their leaves pale from never seeing sunlight. Webbing stretches from root to root, sagging under its own weight. At the center stands the dead trunk itself, split open down the middle like a doorway.

You need a weapon. Searching the room, you find a skeleton slumped against the roots, still in dented plate armor. Across its lap lies a two-handed steel axe, its haft set with gems of ruby, sapphire, and emerald that glitter even in the dark. You lean down and lift it.

Power floods up your arms. *(New power: once per encounter, the gems flare and the axe deals extra radiant damage, burning away webbing within 10 feet.)* Then a voice whispers from the blade: *"You will not perish here. Listen, and I will guide you through this catastrophe."*

Look over the rest of the skeleton. *Make an Investigation check (DC 12).* (On a success:) The straps of its breastplate have rotted through, and the pauldron on its left shoulder comes loose in your hand. It's a curved slab of steel, dented but solid, about the size of a small shield. You loop the old leather strap around your forearm. *(Improvised shield: +1 AC, and it unlocks Shield Slam.)*

You swing the axe before the hollow tree, and the trunk answers. A red glow pulses from deep inside it, and you stumble back a step. A ghost rises out of the light, and it has the same shape as the skeleton at your feet. Its face is gaunt and its eyes burn, and it hovers over you with a presence so heavy that sweat runs down your forehead. Your knees hit the floor.

*"I am Vaelric, last warden of this place,"* it says. *"My axe has chosen you. Carry it where I could not. Ryytaa the Ender nests beyond. Kill her, take what I guarded, and walk out into the light."*

The ghost raises a hand, and the back of the tree splits open, revealing a tunnel.

## Part 4: The Nest

The tunnel ends in a cavern draped in webs, and the floor is littered with egg sacs as big as barrels. Clicking echoes from every direction. Four giant spiders drop from the ceiling on silk lines, and behind them Ryytaa the Ender unfolds, the brood mother herself, as big as a cart, with venom dripping from her fangs. *Roll initiative.*

*Round 1.* The first spider lands right in front of you, its eight eyes shining red in the axe's glow. Its abdomen clenches, its fangs spread wide, and a wet, gurgling sound rises from its throat. It's about to spray venom straight at your face. *Make a Dexterity saving throw (DC 13).* (On a success:) You throw yourself sideways, and the venom spatters across the stone where your head just was, hissing as it eats into the rock. (On a failure:) It catches you across the cheek; take poison damage, and you have disadvantage on your next attack.

(If the player has no shield yet:) As you roll, your hand lands on a curved piece of plate half buried in the webs. You snatch it up just as the spider spits again, and the venom splashes off the steel. *(The player gains the improvised shield now.)*

*Round 2.* You come up swinging. The axe bites into the spider's leg and takes it off, and the spider shrieks and backs away. Two more spiders scuttle down the walls to flank you. *Attack, or brace your shield (the attacking spider has disadvantage).*

*Round 3.* The wounded spider rears up for another spray, but you're already moving. You slam the axe down and the gems burst with red light. The webs around you curl into ash, leaving the spiders nowhere to hide.

*Round 4.* Now Ryytaa the Ender moves. The floor shakes as she charges, her fangs as long as daggers. As she rears back to strike, you step into her and drive the shield into her face with all your weight behind it. *(Shield Slam: Strength contest. On a success, she's stunned until the end of your next turn, and you have advantage on your next attack.)* The steel rings like a bell, and she staggers.

*"Now,"* Vaelric whispers from the axe.

You bring the axe down with both hands. The gems blaze so brightly that the whole nest is lit like noon. When Ryytaa the Ender falls, the egg sacs shrivel, the last spiders flee into the cracks in the walls, and the clicking dies. The shield stays strapped to your arm, scarred by venom and dented in the shape of a spider's face.

## Part 5: The Library of Roots

Past the nest, a door of living wood opens at your touch. Inside, shelves carved into the roots hold rows of spellbooks bound in leather and bark, and a few still hum with magic. Vaelric's voice drifts from the axe: *"Take what you can carry. Knowledge is the only treasure that grows."* *(Give the player 1d3 spellbooks or scrolls of your choice.)*

## Part 6: The Desert

At the back of the library, a spiral stair climbs toward a crack of white light. You climb for what feels like hours, until you push through a stone slab and the heat hits you like a wall. Sand stretches to every horizon, rippling gold beneath a blazing sun. The labyrinth is behind you.

The axe whispers one last time: *"Now the real journey begins."*

## Part 7: The Burning Sands

The slab grinds shut behind you, and when you turn, there is nothing there but a ripple of sand. No stair, no crack of light. The labyrinth has let you go and closed its door.

The sun sits directly overhead, white and merciless. Within minutes your lips crack and the steel of the axe is too hot to touch bare-handed. You wrap the haft in a strip torn from your cloak. Somewhere out here there must be water, or shade, or people. *Make a Survival check (DC 12).* (On a success:) You read the dunes the way you once read the corridors. The wind has piled the sand higher on one side, and far off a line of black specks drifts across the sky. Birds, and birds mean water. (On a failure:) You walk until your shadow stretches long in front of you, then admit you have been walking in a circle. *Take 1d4 heat damage.* Just before dusk, the birds find you instead, circling low over a ridge to the east.

*(Heat rule for the whole desert: every time the player fails a check out in the open sand during daylight, they also take 1 heat damage. HP can't drop below 1, as always.)*

Over the ridge lies a ruined well, ringed by broken pillars of sandstone carved with the same spirals you saw on the labyrinth gate. Someone has strung canvas between two of the pillars for shade. A cookfire smolders. Four figures sit around it, and two of them wear hoods you have seen before.

The taller stranger lowers her hood. She is younger than you expected, with a scar through one eyebrow and a gold coin hanging from a cord around her neck. One of *your* coins.

(If the player gave the strangers three coins in Part 1:) *"Well,"* she says, grinning. *"Nothing that goes down there comes back up. I owe the others a drink."* She tosses you a waterskin. *(Restore 1d6 HP.)*

(If the player brushed past them:) *"The rude one,"* she says. *"Still alive. That's a first."* She doesn't offer water. *Make a Persuasion check (DC 11) to talk your way to a drink.* (On a success: restore 1d6 HP. On a failure: they make you pay. Spend 2 gold, then restore 1d6 HP.)

## Part 8: The Hollow Hands

Her name is Sable, and the four of them call themselves the Hollow Hands. They are thieves, and not apologetic about it.

- **Sable**, the leader, a talker and a planner, who wears your coin like a trophy.
- **Grell**, the shorter stranger from the road, broad and silent, who picks his teeth with a lockpick.
- **Ysolde**, an alchemist with stained fingers and a bandolier of corked glass vials that clink when she moves.
- **Tobbin**, barely more than a boy, who never stops fidgeting with a deck of cards and who watches your axe the whole time.

As night falls and the cold comes in, Sable tells you why they stood on that road. *"Every few years someone goes into the labyrinth. Nobody comes out. We wait by the old well anyway, because the stories say that one day someone will, carrying the warden's axe."* She nods at the gems. *"And there it is."*

Then Vaelric speaks, and for the first time the others can hear him. The gems pulse red, and the fire bends toward the blade. *"Ask her what she wants with it."*

Sable doesn't flinch. *"Three days east is Sulmara, the Glass City. The Magistrate who rules it keeps a vault under his palace that nobody has ever broken. In that vault is a seed the size of your fist, black as coal and warm to the touch. They call it the Ember Root."*

*"My heartwood,"* Vaelric says, and his voice is very quiet. *"They cut it from the great tree while I still lived. Without it, the labyrinth rots. Without it, I cannot rest."*

*"The vault door is warded,"* Sable goes on. *"Old warden magic. Only a warden's blade can open it. We've got the plan, the maps, and the people. We've been missing the key for six years."* She holds out her hand. *"Come with us. Equal share of everything else in that vault, and the seed is yours."*

*If the player has the Warden's Journal, they may read it now: Vaelric's notes confirm that the ward on the vault answers to his axe, and that the Magistrate's family has held the Ember Root for three generations. Gain advantage on the Insight check below.*

*Make an Insight check (DC 13) to read Sable.* (On a success:) She's telling the truth about the vault and the seed. But when she says "equal share," her eyes flick to Tobbin, and Tobbin looks away. Somebody in this crew has their own plan.

**The choice:**

- **Join the Hollow Hands.** Take her hand. *(Go to Part 9A: The Heist.)*
- **Decline.** Tell her you walk alone. *(Go to Part 9B: The Lone Road.)*

## Part 9A: The Heist

Sable spits in her palm and shakes your hand. Grell pins a small iron badge to your collar, an open hand with a hole through the middle. *(New item: the Hollow Hand token. Any member of the crew will vouch for whoever wears it.)*

**The road east.** Three days of walking at night and sleeping in the shade of dunes by day. On the second night a sand-swept ridge collapses under Ysolde and drops her into the burrow of a glass scorpion, its body clear as crystal and its stinger glowing faintly blue. *Roll initiative.* The scorpion has AC 13 and goes down after two hits. If the player uses the gem power, the radiant flare turns the scorpion's crystal body into a prism and blinds it; the next attack has advantage. (On a failed attack, the stinger catches you: *take 1d6 poison damage.*) Ysolde climbs out shaking and presses a vial into your hand. *"I pay my debts."* *(New item: one Smokebloom vial. Shatter it to fill a 20-foot area with thick violet smoke.)*

**Sulmara.** On the third dawn the Glass City rises out of the haze. Its towers are fused sand, green and amber and clear, and they throw the sunrise across the dunes in a hundred colors. At its heart stands the Magistrate's palace, and somewhere under it, the vault.

The crew holes up in the back room of a spice shop. Sable unrolls the map and lays out the plan. The Magistrate holds a masked feast tonight for the merchant lords. The palace will be full of guests, which means full of strangers, which means no one will look twice at five more. The crew goes in three ways, and you choose your part.

*Choose your role in the job:*

- **The Mask.** Walk in the front door with Sable, dressed as a merchant lord, and get the vault key-ring off the Magistrate's steward.
- **The Shadow.** Go over the rooftops with Grell and drop in through the glass dome above the vault stair.
- **The Muscle.** Go in with Tobbin through the servants' tunnels under the kitchens, where the guards are.

**The Mask.** Silk robes, a gilded mask shaped like a hawk, and the axe wrapped in velvet as a "gift for the Magistrate." At the gate, a guard wants to unwrap it. *Make a Deception check (DC 13).* (On a success:) You sigh, explain that it's cursed and only the Magistrate may touch it, and the guard backs away like it might bite. (On a failure:) He unwraps it, and Vaelric glares out of the gems. The guard goes pale and waves you through anyway, but now someone has seen the axe; *the alarm rises one step earlier in the vault scene (the Warden Golem acts first).* Inside, you find the steward, a nervous man with keys at his belt. *Make a Sleight of Hand check (DC 14)*, or charm him: *Persuasion (DC 12)* to get him drunk enough that Sable can lift them. Either way, the key-ring is yours.

**The Shadow.** The rooftops of Sulmara are smooth glass, still warm from the day and slick as ice. *Make an Acrobatics check (DC 13)* to cross from the spice shop to the palace roof. (On a failure: you slide, catch the edge by your fingertips, and Grell hauls you up, but you *take 1d4 damage* and bang the shield against the glass. Make a Stealth check (DC 12) or the guards below look up.) *If the player has the Scroll of Silver Mist, they can skip the rooftops entirely and appear on the palace dome.* Grell cuts a circle in the dome with a diamond-tipped tool, and you lower yourselves on silk rope straight down to the vault stair.

**The Muscle.** The tunnels stink of grease and smoke. Two guards play dice by the kitchen door and stand when they see you. Tobbin flashes a smile and his card deck, and asks if they want a real game. *Make an Intimidation check (DC 12)* to make them fold without a fight, or *attack* (AC 12, each goes down after one hit). (Shield Slam works here: on a success, one guard is knocked into the other and both are out.) Tobbin seems a little too comfortable down here. He knows which doors are locked, and he doesn't need the map.

**The vault.** Whichever way you came, the crew meets at the bottom of a long glass stair. At the end stands a round door of black iron covered in the same druidic spirals as the labyrinth gate. In front of it waits the Warden Golem, nine feet of sandstone and bronze with a lantern of green fire for a heart. It was made to stop anyone who isn't a warden.

*"It knows me,"* Vaelric says. *"It does not know you. Make it know you."*

*Roll initiative.* The Warden Golem has AC 15 and needs three hits to bring down. Each of its turns, it slams a stone fist; *make a Dexterity saving throw (DC 13) or take 1d8 bludgeoning damage.*

- The crew helps: Sable distracts it (your first attack has advantage), Ysolde throws acid (+1 damage die), and Grell braces your back (once, ignore a hit).
- The gems: if the player calls on the gem power, the golem's green lantern flares red in answer, and it hesitates, recognizing its master's light. The golem loses its next turn.
- The talk-down: *If the player has the Warden's Journal or speaks Vaelric's name aloud, they may try an Arcana check (DC 15) instead of fighting. On a success, the golem kneels and lets you pass.*

The axe touches the door, and the spirals light up one by one like a fuse. The door swings open on a round chamber of gold coins, jeweled cups, and silk, and on a pedestal in the middle, under a dome of glass, a black seed glowing like a coal.

**The betrayal.** Before you can reach it, a crossbow bolt cracks into the pedestal an inch from your hand. Tobbin stands in the doorway with his cards gone and a loaded crossbow in his hands. Behind him march a dozen palace guards, and the Magistrate himself, fat and smiling under a mask of beaten gold.

*"The boy's been mine for a year,"* the Magistrate says. *"I've wanted that axe since before he was born. Thank you for bringing it to my door."*

(If the player passed the Insight check in Part 8, they saw this coming. *They may act before anyone else: one free action.* Grab the seed, throw the Smokebloom vial, or put the shield between the bolt and Sable.)

Sable's face goes hard. *"Tobbin. We raised you."* Tobbin won't look at her.

The way out:

- **Smoke and run.** Shatter the Smokebloom vial. In the violet dark, *make a Stealth check (DC 12)* to grab the seed and get the crew back up the glass stair.
- **Fight through.** Use the gem power, if it's still unspent, and the room fills with red light. *Make three attacks (AC 13) to cut a path to the stair.* Each miss *costs 1d6 damage.*
- **Bring the vault down.** *If the player has the Tome of Rootbinding or the Thornwall Grimoire,* cast it. Roots or thorns tear up through the vault floor and seal the doorway between you and the guards. The crew escapes through the dome hole above. No roll needed.
- **Turn Tobbin.** *Make a Persuasion check (DC 15).* Remind him who fed him. (On a success:) Tobbin turns his crossbow on the Magistrate's lantern, the chandelier crashes down, and he comes with you. (On a failure:) He fires; *take 1d8 piercing damage* and choose another way out.

When the glass doors of the palace finally shatter behind you, the crew is running across the rooftops into the night, pockets full of gold, and the Ember Root is warm in your fist. *(Each surviving crew member pays you a share: gain 3d10 gold.)*

*(Go to Part 10: The Ember Root.)*

## Part 9B: The Lone Road

*"Suit yourself,"* Sable says, and the crew lets you walk. But before you go, she presses a waterskin into your hand anyway. *"For the road. And if you change your mind, you'll find us at the sign of the empty hand."*

**The sandstorm.** On the second day the sky turns brown and the wind rises into a howling wall of sand. *Make a Survival check (DC 14)* to find shelter. (On a success:) You dig in behind a dune and wrap your face, and the storm passes over. (On a failure:) The storm scours you raw: *take 2d4 damage.* When it clears, the storm has uncovered something: the top of a buried sandstone arch carved with spirals. *If the player has the Codex of Whispering Bark,* the dried roots in the arch tell them where the old warden road ran. *Gain advantage on the next check in Sulmara.*

**Sulmara.** The Glass City shimmers on the horizon on the third dawn. At the gate a guard sees the axe and the shield, and a different kind of offer finds you. Within the hour, you are standing in the Magistrate's palace in front of the Magistrate himself, fat and smiling under a mask of beaten gold.

*"A traveler with a warden's axe, walking out of the desert,"* he says. *"I have a problem, and you look like the answer. A gang of thieves calling themselves the Hollow Hands means to rob my vault tonight. Guard it, and I'll pay you fifty gold. Catch them, and I'll pay you a hundred."*

*"He is the one who keeps my heartwood,"* Vaelric whispers. *"Say yes. Get close to the vault."*

*Make an Insight check (DC 12).* (On a success:) He already knows exactly how the thieves plan to get in. Someone in the crew is feeding him. He doesn't want to stop the heist at all; he wants the thieves to bring the one thing that opens that door.

**The night of the feast.** You stand guard at the bottom of the long glass stair, before the round black iron door and the Warden Golem, which stands silent and still in front of it. At midnight, the Hollow Hands come down the stair: Sable in a hawk mask, Grell from the dome above, Tobbin from the tunnels. When Sable sees you, her face falls. *"Ah,"* she says. *"So that's who he hired."*

Then Tobbin turns and levels a crossbow at her back, and the Magistrate and a dozen guards fill the stair behind him. *"Well done,"* the Magistrate says to you. *"Now take her."*

**The choice:**

- **Side with the Magistrate.** You do the job you were paid for. *Make two attacks (AC 13)* to subdue Sable and Grell; they don't fight back hard. The Magistrate pays you a hundred gold, then orders you to hand over the axe. When you refuse, the Warden Golem lumbers to life at his command. *(Fight the golem as written in Part 9A, alone, without the crew's help.)* When it falls, the Magistrate flees, and the vault door stands before you with no one left to stop you. *(Gain 100 gold, and the Hollow Hands will remember you as an enemy.)*
- **Side with the crew.** You throw the shield up between Tobbin's bolt and Sable's back. The bolt rings off the steel. *"Change of plans,"* you tell her. Now you fight the guards together. *Make a Shield Slam (Strength contest DC 13)* to knock Tobbin down the stair. Then use the axe on the door while Sable and Grell hold the stairway, and choose a way out as in Part 9A (smoke and run, fight through, bring the vault down). *(Sable takes the Hollow Hand token from Tobbin's collar and pins it on yours. Gain 2d10 gold from the vault.)*
- **Take it for yourself.** While the guards and thieves clash, you slip past them all. *Make a Stealth check (DC 15)*, then touch the axe to the door. The spirals light, the door swings open, and you snatch the Ember Root from its pedestal and run. *(Gain the Ember Root but no gold. Both the Magistrate and the Hollow Hands now hunt you.)*

*(Go to Part 10: The Ember Root.)*

## Part 10: The Ember Root

You stop running on a dune outside the city where the glass towers are only a glow against the night. The seed is warm in your hand, and it beats softly, like a heart.

Vaelric's ghost rises out of the axe, clearer now than he has been since the hollow tree. His face isn't gaunt anymore. He looks like a man, not a corpse. *"You did what I could not,"* he says. *"Now plant it where the great tree fell, and the labyrinth will heal. Or keep it, and it will grow in you instead."*

*(The GM explains the Ember Root's power: whoever carries it regains 1 HP at every sunrise and may cast one druidic spell per day from any book they carry, with no roll.)*

**The final choice:**

- **Return the seed.** Walk back west to the well and the spiral arch, and bury the seed in the sand where the labyrinth lies below. Roots burst up through the dunes, green for the first time in a hundred years, and Vaelric smiles. *"Rest well, warden,"* he says, and you realize he means you. The gems on the axe go dark, but the axe stays sharp and the labyrinth will open for you whenever you return. *(Vaelric is at peace. The player is now the Warden of the Labyrinth: once per day, they may ask the roots for directions.)*
- **Keep the seed.** Close your fist. The warmth runs up your arm and into your chest, and something takes root under your ribs. Vaelric doesn't argue. *"Then carry us both,"* he says, and sinks back into the axe. *(The player gains the Ember Root's power permanently. Vaelric stays bound to the axe as a voice that grows quieter every day.)*

If you rode with the Hollow Hands, Sable finds you at sunrise, sitting on the dune with Grell, Ysolde, and maybe Tobbin. (If you gave her three coins on the road, she tosses them back to you.) *"The road out is long,"* she says. *"We've got another job in the Salt Marshes. Interested?"*

The axe is quiet. The sand is gold, and the sun is rising.

## The Hushfen: What the Mire Keeps

*The swamp past the Salt Marshes has a saying attached to it, and every fisherman on the coast knows it: "Nothing the Hushfen drowns stays dead." A whole kingdom went under that mire a hundred years ago, and the fishermen say it is still down there, waiting.*

*(Conversions for the GM: Adrian's chances are turned into d20 checks. A 77% chance is a DC 6, a 50% chance is a DC 11, a 56% chance is a DC 10, and a 34% chance is a DC 14. HP still can't drop below 1, and the Strangers' Blessing still gives one reroll.)*

## Part 11: The Blackjackal's Bounty

However the night in Sulmara ended, Tobbin ran. The Magistrate's men are hunting him, and so is everyone he ever lied to. He took one thing with him when he went: a folded square of yellow vellum that Sable had kept sewn into the lining of her pack for six years.

Sable finds you on the coast road where the dunes give way to grey salt flats. (If you rode with the Hollow Hands, she is already beside you. If you didn't, she walks up to your fire with her hands open and empty. *"Truce,"* she says. *"The boy robbed us both."*)

She draws a copy of the vellum in the sand with her knife. It is a bounty writ, sealed in the wax of a kingdom that no longer exists.

*"A hundred years ago,"* she says, *"there was a kingdom called Carrowmere, where the Hushfen is now. Green fields, a white castle, a king who taxed the roads. And on those roads there was a robber they called the Blackjackal. Nobody saw his face. He wore a black jackal's hide over his head and shoulders, and he could stand in the dark next to you and you'd never know. He robbed the king's tax wagons for twenty years and laughed about it."*

*"The king sent his best knight after him. Ser Oswin Harrowgate, the Lantern Knight, they called him, because he carried a lamp on his shield that never went out. Oswin chased the Jackal for a year and finally cornered him on the night of the spring floods, at the edge of the fens, with the whole stolen treasury of Carrowmere loaded on a barge behind him."*

She taps the writ. *"Nobody knows what happened next. The floods came that night and never left. The fen swallowed the castle, the fields, the knight, the robber, and every crown of the treasury. Carrowmere drowned. The king put this bounty on the Jackal's head the week before, and nobody ever collected it. But the writ is stamped with the royal seal of Carrowmere, and the old stories say the seal is the key to the drowned treasury."*

*"Tobbin knows it too. And he ran into the Hushfen with it three days ago."*

Sable can't follow you. *"The Hushfen lets one walker in at a time,"* she says. *"Two go in, one comes out. Everybody on this coast knows that."* She gives you a coil of rope and a lantern. *"Bring back the writ. Bring back the boy if you can. I want to ask him why."*

## Part 12: The Soggy Mile

The salt flats turn to reeds, the reeds turn to black water, and then you are in the Hushfen.

You walk for two hours. The ground gives under every step. Mire trees rise out of the water on knotted roots like stilts, their bark grey and soft as wet paper, and long beards of moss hang down from their branches to the water. Things move in that water. Frogs as big as dogs watch you from the logs. Once, a heron taller than you lifts out of the reeds without a sound. Between the hummocks of solid ground lie flat, innocent-looking patches of bright green sand, and every one of them is quicksand.

*Make a Perception check (DC 6).*

(On a success:) You read the mire. You step only on the dark roots and the humps of grass, you smell the sulfur rising off the sinkholes and keep your distance, and you notice the long silver trails across the logs before you put your hand on one. Something big lives out here, and it leaves slime wherever it goes. You give the trails a wide berth and keep walking.

(On a failure:) Your boot goes through a skin of green sand and you sink to the knee, then to the hip. By the time you've dragged yourself out by a mire root, you've made a great deal of noise. Behind you, from the reeds, comes a wet, slow sound like a sack being dragged across stone. Then another. Then more.

**The dissolvers.** Three slugs slide out of the reeds, each one as long as a horse, their grey-green bodies glistening, their eyestalks swiveling toward you. Where they cross the logs, the wood smokes and softens. The slime they leave behind eats armor and flesh alike, and they are coming faster than anything that big should move.

*(The slugs: AC 10, each goes down after one hit. Each slug's turn: it spits a gob of dissolving slime. Make a Dexterity saving throw (DC 12) or take 1d6 acid damage. The first time slime hits you, it also eats through the strap and face of your improvised shield: -1 AC until someone mends it.)*

You can fight them with the axe, but the slime will eat the steel if you hit too often. This is a fight for magic. *Roll initiative,* and choose how to fight:

- **A spell.** Read from one of your books. *Make a spell attack roll (d20 against the slug's AC 10).* On a hit, the spell kills one slug.
  - *Scroll of Moonfire:* a beam of silver flame boils the slug where it lies. On a hit, the beam is long enough to catch a second slug behind it.
  - *Tome of Rootbinding:* the mire roots wrap the slugs and drag them under the black water. On a hit, all three are held still for a round, and every attack against them has advantage.
  - *The Thornwall Grimoire:* a wall of thorns rises between you and the slugs. On a hit, they have to crawl through the thorns, and the thorns shred them. Two slugs die.
  - *Scroll of Silver Mist:* you vanish and reappear on a high root 30 feet away. You don't kill anything, but you can skip this fight entirely and keep walking.
  - *Codex of Whispering Bark:* the mire trees tell you where the slugs are soft: just behind the eyestalks. Your next two attacks have advantage.

## Part 14: Thirteen Years in the Fen

*Make a Persuasion check (DC 12)* to get the Baron to tell his story.

**Varro's memory.** *(Read this aloud as a flashback.)*

Thirteen years ago, Varro Duskwane was a lord of Sulmara, with a glass tower and a fleet of salt barges and a seat on the merchant council. He was also the only lord on that council who would not pay the Magistrate's bribes.

He walked into the mire to die. He sank to the chest in the green sand, and he was letting it take him, when the shadows of the mire trees reached down and pulled him out. A voice in the dark spoke to him. It was low and dry and laughing. *"Another robbed man. Good. I could use one."*

## Carrowmere: The Drowned Castle

## Part 18: The Crossing

  - (On a failure:) It takes the food, and the oar with it, and bumps the boat hard on the way past. *Take 1d4 damage.* You paddle the rest of the way with your hands.
- **Fight it.** *Make three attack rolls (AC 13).* Each miss means the Gravepike slams the boat: *take 1d6 damage.* Spells work here: *Moonfire, Rootbinding (the lakeweed grabs it),* or *Thornwall* counts as one automatic hit, and the gems give advantage on one swing. (On a failure:) *"You lie as badly as he did,"* he says, and raises the shield. *(Take 1d8 damage, then fight him.)*
- **Free him from his vow.** *(Only if you know the secret.)* Tell him what the king did. *Make a Persuasion check (DC 8, advantage with the Warden's Journal).* (On a success:) Oswin is quiet for a long time. *"I knew,"* he says at last. *"I think I always knew. I just couldn't bear to have drowned for nothing."* He takes off his helm, and there's a face inside it for one moment, a tired, decent face. *(On a failure, he grieves and turns away, but he still steps aside. The truth is the truth.)*

However it ends, Oswin unfastens the lantern from his shield and holds it out to you. *(If you already carry his lamp, he lights it from this one instead, and the two flames burn as one.)* *"It shows what's hidden,"* he says. *"Take it down there. I don't want to see it again."* Then he sits down by the window and puts his hands on his knees, and, slowly, the two small lights behind his visor go out.

(If Vaelric still speaks to you:) *"Rest, old friend,"* he says. *"You kept your oath longer than any of us."*

## Part 22: The Drowned Treasury

Below the castle, below the lake, a stair spirals down to a round iron door. It is carved with a crown, a lantern, and a jackal's head.

**The first lock: the seal.** Press the wax seal on the writ to the crown. *(Or the Royal Signet, if you took it from Tobbin.)* The door grinds open. Far above you, something groans: the old floodgates, waking up. Water begins to pour down the stair behind you.

**The second lock: the light.** The next hall is dark and knee-deep and rising. Hold up Oswin's lantern. The light shows what's hidden: a path of safe stones across a floor full of pit traps, and a keyhole hidden in the eye of a carved fish. *Make an Investigation check (DC 12; advantage with the Warden's Journal or with Tobbin's help).* (On a failure:) You find it, but not before you go through the floor into the pit to your chest. *Take 1d6 damage* hauling yourself out as the water climbs.

**The third lock: the question.** The water is at your chest now. The last door has a jackal's head carved in it, and when you come near, its jaws open and it speaks in a dry, laughing voice. *"Whose gold is this?"*

- *"The king's."* or *"The Jackal's."* — The jaws snap shut. The water roars in over your head. *Make an Athletics check (DC 12)* to hold your breath and force the door. *(On a failure, take 2d4 damage.)* The door gives either way.
- *"The people's."* — *(This is the right answer. If you know the secret, you know it.)* The jaws laugh, and the door swings open, and the water stops rising.

**The vault.** The treasury of Carrowmere lies under a dome of air: chests split open and spilling crowns, gold plates, a silver throne for a child, rubies in heaps like red beans. *Fill your pack. Gain 4d10 gold.* (If you broke the chains in the counting house, King Aldous is here, on his knees in the coins, trying to gather them up with hands that pass through them. When the lantern light touches him, he crumbles away like wet sand.)

As you fill your pack, the lantern flickers. The shadows in the vault lean in. And out of them steps the Blackjackal: a lean man in a black jackal's hide (or Tobbin, smiling his new smile, if you handed him over) with nothing behind his eyes but the dark.

*"A hundred years,"* he says, *"waiting for someone to put out that lantern."* He scoops up a single coin, flips it to you, and grins. *"The gold was always meant for the robbed. Do some good with it. As for me, there's a Magistrate in a glass city who has robbed more people than any king. I think I'll pay him a visit."*

He steps backward into the shadow and is gone.

You climb up out of the vault and the castle as the lake finally finishes swallowing it. When you reach the boat, the lantern in the tower window is dark. (If you warned Varro, he's waiting for you on the far shore, wrapped in violet and frowning at the water. *"So,"* he says. *"He's free. Then so am I. Sulmara won't know what hit it."*)

## Lastwell: The Hunted Village

*Parts XXIII–XXVIII pick up on the road east from the lake, toward Sulmara. This chapter is about survival. The player tracks water, food, exhaustion and the Hunt, and is stalked through a dead village by a thing nobody can see.*

### How Lastwell plays

**Water and food.** The lake has spoiled the rations: the player starts with 2 water and 1 meal. Every morning (the start of Parts XXIV, XXV, XXVII and XXVIII), drink 1 water and eat 1 meal. Each one missed adds a level of exhaustion. Eating and drinking both removes one level and restores 1d4 HP. Foul water can be drunk, but needs a Constitution save (DC 12) against ooze sickness. It can be made clean by boiling (needs a torch or the Lantern Knight's lamp; Hunt +1 for the smoke) or by filtering it through charcoal (Survival DC 13; on a failure the water is lost).

**Exhaustion (0–5).** Each level adds to the one before. 1: disadvantage on ability checks. 2: disadvantage on attack rolls. 3: the Wringer has advantage on attacks. 4: HP maximum halved. 5: no healing.

**Ooze sickness.** You need two drinks a day, and take 1d4 poison damage each morning. Cured by feverfew, by comb honey, or by sweating it out (Medicine DC 15).

**Healing.** Lastwell is short on it, and all of it is found:
- **Draught of Mending** (regain 10 HP): in Ilse's satchel, on the herbwife's top shelf (on a Medicine success), in Hobb's tool roll, and in Maud's stash.
- **Elixir of Health** (regain 20 HP): in the reeve's strongbox (if you solve the cellar seal), and in Maud's stash.
- **Gravemoss poultices**: moss grows only on the old graves in the churchyard, the one ground the Wringer never drank. You get 1 from the herbwife (on a success), 3 in the churchyard at the Belfry, and 2 more if you go back during the Smithy (Hunt +1). Bind one with a Medicine check (DC 10): on a success regain 2d6 HP now and 1d4 more the next morning; on a failure regain 1d4.
- You're offered these each morning and after every pass of the Wringer. In a fight, using one costs your turn. At exhaustion 5, nothing heals you.

**The Hunt (0–10).** Noise raises it: searching, hammering, the winch, a ringing bell, falling into the ooze. At 10 the Wringer comes, and the Hunt resets to 2 after each pass.

**A pass of the Wringer.** Make a Perception check (DC 13, advantage with the lantern) to hear it coming. If you hear it, you have a few heartbeats to lay up to three rune mines, scatter caltrops, and hide (Stealth DC 15, advantage with Shadowblend) or stand. If you don't hear it, it gets a free attack. It attacks at +6 for 1d8 necrotic damage, and it's unseen, so your attacks have disadvantage until something reveals it. Two hits in a row cost a level of exhaustion. The Wringer has 60 HP. Wounds carry over between passes and it drinks back 4 each morning, but it can't be killed before the finale: at 10 HP it pulls back to drink.

**Snare rune mines.** Ilse Marrow's scrolls: **Emberbite** (fire, 3d6: reveals it), **Blightbloom** (poison, 2d6: it takes 1d6 every time it wrings you, for the rest of the chapter), **Rimefang** (cold, 2d8: slows it, so its attacks have disadvantage). Each placement is an Arcana check (DC 12, advantage with Snare Trap). On a failure the scroll fizzles. On a natural 1, or a miss by 5 or more, the mine goes off on you. Mines are laid in rings and go off in order, outer to inner:
- Rimefang then Emberbite: the ice shatters, and the fire does double damage.
- Blightbloom then Emberbite: the gas catches, for +2d6.
- Emberbite then Blightbloom: the fire burns the rot away, so the poison does half damage and has no effect.
- **It learns.** Each repeat of a rune does half damage, and from the third use it ignores that rune's effect.

**Scribing more scrolls.** Each scroll takes 1 blank parchment and 1 measure of ooze ink (scraped from the puddles, 2 measures each time, Hunt +1). Scribing is a memory puzzle: watch the rune's strokes, then trace them back. Emberbite has 4 strokes, Blightbloom 5, Rimefang 6. A wrong stroke spoils the ink, and sometimes the ink bites (1d6 necrotic).

**Caltrops.** These need *Hobb's Book of Iron Mischief*, 2 scrap iron per bag, and a lit forge. Make a Dexterity check (DC 12). A total of 18 or more makes **fine** caltrops: it's slowed, and it takes 1d4 every time it attacks. Otherwise they're **crude** and only slow it. If the book was soaked in the ooze, you can only make crude ones. Each batch raises the Hunt by 2.

**The well-keeper's lock.** Four rings, six runes each. The answer, Bell · Root · Hand · Eye, is scattered across the village. Every wrong haul tells you how many rings caught, and raises the Hunt by 2.

## Part 23: The Puddles

The road east climbs through two days of empty hills. On the first night you open your pack, and the lake has been in it: the bread is green, the dried meat soft and sweet-smelling. One heel of cheese and a skin of lake water are left. *(Water 2, food 1.)*

On the second evening, a milestone: LASTWELL ½ MILE. There's no smoke and no dogs. The first villager is on the road by an overturned handcart: a carter's coat laid flat, boots still laced, and in the middle of them a puddle of grey ooze the color of dishwater, with a wedding ring in it. The flies won't land on it. There are more in doorways, on benches, in a cradle on a porch.

By the well in the square lies a puddle that is still warm, in a rune-scribe's blue robe. Her satchel holds **six Snare Rune scrolls** (2 Emberbite, 2 Blightbloom, 2 Rimefang), 3 blank parchment, an inkhorn and a journal. She was **Ilse Marrow of Sulmara**. Her last entry: the well went dry at midsummer, and Corwen the well-digger went down after the water and came back up three days later. Now people sleep and wake as puddles. She thinks the thing goes back down the well to pour out what it drinks. The well-keeper, Old Maud, locked the well with four rings, and told Ilse the first: **the Bell**. *(Investigation DC 10: a note in the back gives the mine combinations.)*

On the inside of the inn door, scratched with fingernails: **THE WRINGER. DON'T SLEEP. DON'T SLEEP.** *(The Hunt starts at 3.)*

**Scavenge.** Choose up to three:
- **The inn kitchen** (Hunt +1). All the food has gone grey. *Investigation DC 13:* a sealed crock of pickled eggs, *+1 food*. On a failure you touch a "cheese" that gives like a blister, and make a *Constitution save (DC 10)* against ooze sickness.
- **The beer cellar** (Hunt +2). *Athletics DC 13:* the one clean cask at the top of the stack, *+2 water*. On a failure the stack comes down: *1d6.*
- **The cooper's yard** (Hunt +1). Iron hoops: *+2 iron.*
- **The herbwife's cottage** (Hunt +1). *Medicine DC 12:* *2 sprigs of feverfew.*

At midnight, in the barred taproom, the bar on the door creaks as something leans on it. **First pass**, forewarned: you have time to lay mines.

## Part 24: The Well and the Bell

*Morning upkeep.* The puddles have skinned over in the sun. Choose any number of the following, and pay the Hunt for each:
- **The well** (Hunt +2). Capped with an iron lid and the four-ring lock, but the bucket hole is open. *+3 foul water,* so thick it holds the bucket's shape.
- **The cracked bell.** It fell mouth-up in the broken belfry and has filled with rain. *Athletics DC 14:* *+2 water*, and around its lip, sealed in red wax, the second ring: **the Root**. On a failure, *1d6* and the bell rings: *Hunt +4.*
- **The reeve's cellar.** *Puzzle:* nine rune stones; each press turns a stone and its neighbors. Light all nine (every 4 presses, Hunt +1). Solved: *+3 food, +1 water* (cider), and feverfew if you have none. Or pry it open: *Athletics DC 16, Hunt +3*, the ward breaks and the cold gets in first: *+1 food.*
- **The goat.** Half wrung, still breathing. *Medicine DC 13* to cut only the good meat: *+2 food.* On a failure you still get the food, but make a *Constitution save (DC 13)* against ooze sickness.
- **The chimney hive.** Needs fire: a torch or the lantern, or burn an Emberbite scroll. *Dexterity DC 12* or *2d4 poison* from stings. Gain **comb honey**, which counts as a meal and cures ooze sickness.
- **Gather ooze ink** (Hunt +1), and **purify** any foul water.

You choose the church for the night, and open the door on a nave that is a grey lake, knee-deep, where every family sheltered. Hanging from the broken spire on a chain is a gibbet cage with something in it. You sleep on the step. **A pass of the Wringer.**

## Part 25: The Gibbet Spire

*Morning upkeep.* In the cage: a tinker's coat, a tool roll, and a book chained to the bars, over a thin grey drip that has run down out of the cage into the nave for days. Ilse's journal: *"Hobb the tinker says the Wringer can't climb."* It reached him anyway.

- **Climb the bell rope and cross the rafters.** *Athletics DC 12* for the rope, then the **rafters puzzle**: a 4 × 5 grid of beams. Step one board at a time. Each board you stand on shows how many rotten boards touch it. Tapping a board with the axe haft tells you if it's rotten (Hunt +1 per tap), and the lantern shows two rotten boards. A rotten board drops you into the nave: *2d6*, a *Constitution save (DC 13)* against ooze sickness, *Hunt +3*, then climb again. The board you fell through is now known.
- **Throw the axe at the chain** from the loft (*attack vs AC 17*; Hunt +2 per miss). The cage falls into the ooze and you wade after it (*Constitution DC 14*, *Hunt +3*). The book is **soaked**: crude caltrops only.
- **Freeze the nave first** with a Rimefang scroll (*Arcana DC 13*), then throw. The cage lands on ice and the book stays dry.

In the cage: **Hobb's Book of Iron Mischief**, *+2 iron* (horseshoe nails), and burned into the cover, the third ring: **the Hand**. *"Fourth, ask the smith, he cut it."* Then the whole grey lake in the nave moves toward the door, a slow wave, as something steps into it from the far end. **A pass of the Wringer.**

## Part 26: The Belfry

*A recovery phase.* After the pass in the nave, you climb up to the broken belfry. Nobody died this high, so there are no puddles, and the Wringer travels through its puddles: it can't reach you here. *(The Hunt falls to 0.)*

**Rest.** You sleep properly for the first time in days: *regain 2d6 HP*, *exhaustion −1*, and the rain fills the bell again: *+1 water*. *(With Snare Trap: Survival check at your snare DC to catch a crow at dawn, +1 food. The crows eat up high, not down there.)*

**The Scroll of Revelation.** In the candle niche behind the bell, there's a scroll sealed in yellow wax with an open eye. Reading it burns the words off the page, and you see Lastwell as it was, with every hiding place lit from inside. The brightest light is under the churchyard, in the Lastwell family crypt. *Make an Arcana check (DC 13).* On a success you see Old Maud carrying sacks down into the dark weeks ago: she knew it was coming. On a failure you see too much, every hiding person and the grey coming up under each of them: *1d6 psychic*. Either way you know where to dig.

**The secret food stash.** The churchyard is the only ground in Lastwell with no puddles. Behind a plaque carved by an unskilled hand, *MAUD LASTWELL, WELL-KEEPER*, with no date, is a door. *Athletics DC 12* to shift the stone (on a failure it booms: Hunt +2). Behind it is a larder cut into the earth: oats, peas, hams in salt barrels, sealed jugs, salt everywhere and a thick white line of it across the threshold. *+4 food, +2 water.* Maud is there, a puddle on a stool inside a ring of salt she poured around herself. The ring is perfect except in one place, where her heel scuffed through it in her sleep.

Gain **Maud's Salt**: the next time the Wringer comes, pour a ring of it and you hear it coming automatically. *(If you have ooze sickness, the salt pork gives a Constitution save, DC 12, to sweat it out.)*

## Part 27: The Smithy

*Morning upkeep.* Stone walls, an iron door, and the smith a puddle by the anvil, his hammer still in it. Stamped into the anvil's horn: the fourth ring, **the Eye**.

- **Coffin nails** at the undertaker's (Hunt +2). The only dead in Lastwell who still have faces. *+3 iron.*
- **Horseshoes** from the stable. The horses are puddles from the withers down, with their heads on the straw. *+2 iron*; *Wisdom save (DC 12)* or gain a level of exhaustion.
- **Light the forge** (Hunt +2). Needs a torch or the lantern, or burn an Emberbite scroll.
- **Make caltrops**, **scribe scrolls**, **gather ink**, **boil water.**

Bar the door. It doesn't use the door: near midnight, the smith's puddle draws itself up into a grey column, leans toward you, and falls away into nothing. It goes where its puddles are. **Two passes**: the first forewarned, the second before dawn. At first light you hear it in the square, pouring itself out into the well, over and over.

## Part 28: The Wringing

*Morning upkeep.* The lock doesn't keep the Wringer in. It keeps everyone else out of the only place it has to go. **The winch puzzle:** set four rings. Each wrong haul shows how many rings caught (Hunt +2). Or break it: *Athletics DC 16, Hunt +3*, and on a failure *1d6* from a flying shard.

Ring the well with up to **four mines** (Arcana DC 11: you have time), scatter caltrops, and bait it: your blood (*1d4*), your last clean water, or nothing (then it comes up on the side you weren't watching, and gets a free attack).

At nightfall it climbs out of the well and through your rings. Then fight until it falls. Below 20 HP it's staggering at the lip of the well: **Shield Slam** (+5) or **shove** it (+3), contested against its +4 (+1 if slowed), to throw it down.

Then you see it: a tall, stooped man in skin like a wet sack, eyeless, with arms too long and lined from wrist to armpit with small round mouths that open and close like gills. A well-digger's lamp hangs on a cord at its neck. **Corwen.** It goes down the shaft like a man down a ladder, and everything it drank out of Lastwell pours out of it in a roaring flood that rises up the shaft and stops just below the lip. The water is clean and cold. You drink until you're sick. *(Water to 6, foul water gone, exhaustion −2.)*

Nothing else changes. The puddles stay puddles. You walk out past the carter's coat and you don't take the ring. (If Vaelric still speaks to you:) *"The wardens would have burned it to the ground. The whole village. I think they would have been right."*

Two days east, the road crests a ridge, and Sulmara glitters in the valley below like broken glass.

## The Glass Assize

*Parts XXIX–XXXIV.*

Sulmara again, the Glass City you robbed in the desert chapter. The old Magistrate vanished from his palace eleven nights ago (the Blackjackal paid him the visit he promised). The High Justiciar, **Hesper Coyle, the Glass Judge**, now rules the city. In three days she'll try you at the Glass Assize for his disappearance, with Tobbin as her star witness. If you're found guilty, the sun-mirror burns you at noon.

### Systems

- **Heat (0–10).** Being seen raises it. Each outing costs 1–2, failed checks and loud mistakes cost more, and Streetwise takes 1 off every outing. A night at the inn brings it down by 3. At 10, the Glass Wardens come: you can run the rooftops (Acrobatics DC 14), fight two wardens, or pay 10 gold. Heat then resets to 5, and the Merchant Lords hear about the chase.
- **Six turns.** You get two days, each with a morning, afternoon and night. There's more to do than there are turns, so you have to choose.
- **Disguises** are sold by Feeny, in the inn's cellar. The glazier's leathers get you into the Lens Tower. The debt-collector's chain gets you into the Debtors' Cells. The pilgrim's veil gets you through checkpoints and earns +1 with the Temple jurors.
- **The jury** is 12 jurors on 4 benches: Glaziers, Merchant Lords, Temple of the Clear Flame and Debtors' Commons. Each bench starts at 1 of 3, and you need 7 of 12 to walk free. The jury meter shows on the sheet during the trial.

### Places

- **Vitreous Library** (by day). Brother Orrin, a blind archivist, reads glass books by touch. Here you can:
  - **Buy skill books**:
    - Lockcraft (20 gold)
    - Jurisprudence (25 gold)

    - Streetwise (10 gold)
    - Glasswright, a crafting skill (22 gold)
    - Herbcraft, a crafting skill (18 gold)
  - **Do two readings per visit** (Investigation DC 12):
    - The Assize Rolls show which bench each argument will move.

    - On Witness Glass makes the mended pane count double with the Glaziers.
  - **Ask Orrin** for the cipher hint.
- **The Cracked Lantern inn.** Marrit Quell runs it. You can rest at night (2d6 HP, Heat −3), take a hot bath (2 gold), buy herb bundles, and craft at the bench with Glasswright (shatter flasks, burning lens) or Herbcraft (Draughts of Mending). The job board lists the escort missions. Varro (if you warned him) and Ysolde (if you're friends with the Hollow Hands) visit at night.

1. **Witness pane.** A 3×3 sliding picture. Lockcraft shows the shard numbers. The watchman passes every 20 moves (Heat +1). Fallback: carry the shards out.

- **Night talks** happen on the inn roof. On the second night, at fondness 3 or more, "Stay anyway" leads to a kiss and the romance.
- **In the finale,** if her fondness is at least 1 you can call her in for a 2d6+2 stab. At 3 or more she takes a gavel blow for you. The ending changes with her fondness.

## The Corrach Three: Thrumhold

This chapter follows The Glass Assize. Brother Orrin's glass map shows that the oldest road down to the second Sulmara starts three days north of the city, under a grey mountain called **Corrach**. The valley under Corrach is home to three settlements, known together as "the Three":

- **Ashgate**, a salt-and-copper mining town at the foot of the mountain. It has the bounty board and the bank.
- **Vellum Rest**, a hillside village where the Bloom-sick live. You learn the cures and the Hesk language here.
- **Thrumhold**, the fortress of the Hesk, hidden inside the mountain.

### The Hesk

Adrian asked for something new instead of carrion beetles, so the race is an original one.

- **What they are.** The Hesk are tall, humanoid cicadas (not a hive, beetles or spiders). They stand taller than a door, with shell the color of bark, wide-set eyes like smoked glass, and folded wings too small to fly with.
- **How they live.** They sleep in stone cradles for seventeen years, then wake for one summer to sing and to trade **moltwater** for salt at Vellum Rest.
- **How they speak.** They talk in a droning chest-song made of four tones: low drone, rasp, click and high whine.
- **Why they're dangerous now.** They woke four years early. The Blackjackal dragged the Magistrate through their sleeping halls on his way down the deep stair, and his passing cracked a hundred cradles. Korr-Vesh, **the Long Drummer**, woke first and woke sick. Ever since, he has beaten the great drum to keep everyone awake and raiding.
- **Song-forged weapons (Adrian's lore).** The Hesk channel sound through the Bloom fungus and shape it with resonance until it hardens into a weapon. A gleaner hums its hook-glaive into being on the run, and the blade slumps back into white fur when the song stops. Ninefold eats porridge with a spoon he sang out of fungus. On Thrumhold's third tier, a choir-forge of Hesk sings glaives up out of a bed of fungus. Korr-Vesh hums the Bloom off his own arms onto his drum-bones as barbed points, and his drum-calls rain hardened shards. Thessk sings a training blade out of shell-sand, and the Moltglaive was sung from moltwater salt, which is why it carries no Bloom.
- **Their weapons.** They carry song-forged hook-glaives, spore-censers and spore-hounds.
- **Fighting the song.** The Drum of Stilling makes their song falter, so their blades soften and hit for 2 less for the rest of the fight. At song 2 you can sing a discord into a Hesk blade (Wisdom DC 13, once a fight). The blade crumbles, and the Hesk loses its next turn singing a new one from bare shell. That one is clean, so its hits don't carry the Bloom for the rest of the fight.
- **Named Hesk:**
  - Skarrit, the raid-leader
  - Thessk, champion of the Ring of Echoes
  - Iyu, the Old Cantor, who sings the Long Sleep and is held prisoner
  - Ninefold, an exile who lives at Vellum Rest and teaches you their song

### The Pale Bloom (the disease that stops the axe's revive)

- **Catching it.** Hesk blades, spore-hounds, spore pockets and censers all call for a Constitution save (+2, DC 11–14). Each failure raises the Bloom one stage.
- **Stage 1, Spotted.** Vaelric's axe can't keep you up at 0 HP. Fungus eats roots, and his power is roots.
- **Stage 2, Rooted.** All healing is halved, including potions and rest.
- **Stage 3, Blooming.** You lose 1d4 HP every morning and at the start of every part.
- **Dying.** If you hit 0 HP while you carry the Bloom, you die. You wake at Vellum Rest at full HP with the Bloom at stage 1, and you lose half the gold in your purse (gold in the bank is safe). Sister Wren gives you a Draught of Mending if you have none. Then the part replays from your last rest, which is the start of the part or the last time slot.
- **Counters:**
  - Saltroot tea: 4 gold at Vellum Rest, or brew it yourself with Herbcraft. Each cup is −1 stage.
  - Wren's salt poultice: −1 stage, once a day.
  - The smelter's firebath: 3 gold, or free once you kill the salamander. −1 stage, once a day.
  - The Saltroot Mask: every save is 3 easier.
  - The Weathercloak's poison thread: advantage on every save.
  - **Moltwater** from the cistern in Thrumhold: a full cure, and you can't catch the Bloom again.

### Systems

- **Time.** You have three days in Ashgate, each with a morning, afternoon and night, so nine turns. Errands in town (the bank, the tailor, the firebath, the shop, drinking tea) take no time.

  - The final boss has 30 + 2×drum HP.

  - **Wat Gorse** (any time). He steals the Bloom-sick for the Hesk. Pays 12 gold alive or 8 dead. Track him with Investigation and Survival, then fight him. You free two villagers and find his tally book, the proof that the Reeve is paying him.
  - **Corvin Dray, the Thrice-Seen** (by day). A highwayman, 25 gold. In each round of the fight you have to pick the real Corvin out of three in a shell game. The Lantern Knight's lamp shows you which one throws a shadow. Pays the Mirrorwake blade and the Tarn treasure map.
  - **Skarrit** (by night). You fight a spore-hound, then Skarrit himself. Pays 20 gold, the spore-hound pelt (the poison hide), the location of the Singing Door, and drum −2.
  - **The quiet contract** (an assassination). It comes through the bank and is open to account holders only: 40 gold, paid into your account, to kill Reeve Halloran. Investigating shows he has been feeding the sick to the Hesk. You can kill him, expose him in the square (25 gold and drum −1), or take his 30-gold bribe, after which Sister Wren charges you double.
  - **The Thrumhold Contract.** It unlocks after 2 bounties or after Skarrit. Pays 60 gold to collapse the drum hall, and comes with 2 blasting charges.

He also runs a dead depositor's box auction for 8 gold, which holds the Slag-Field map, and he passes on the quiet contract.

  - Song 1: Hesk attack you at −1, you can read the cache map, and you can understand Skarrit and Iyu.
  - Song 2: you can talk past guards, ask for moltwater, challenge in the Ring of Echoes, break Korr-Vesh's drum calls, and learn the Long Sleep from Iyu.

  - The Tarn Pay-Chest (from Corvin's coat): 20 gold and an Elixir of Health.
  - The Seventeen-Year Cache (Ninefold's tablet, readable at song 1, in Thrumhold's oldest tier): the Seventeen-Year Ring.

## The Kept Noon: Ammaret (Parts XLII–XLVIII)

This chapter follows The Corrach Three. The deep stair under Thrumhold goes down for thousands of steps to **Ammaret**, the old Sulmara. Six hundred years ago the desert melted to glass over it, and the new Sulmara was built on top of that glass. From below, the sky is amber glass from wall to wall, and you can see the dark soles of Sulmara's streets through it.

### The Lidless and the Kept Noon

- **The Kept Noon.** Before the glass came down, Ammaret's Noonkeepers caught the last noon in a lens as big as a house, at the top of a lighthouse tower, so their city would never be dark. It has been noon in Ammaret ever since. Every clock in the city says twelve.
- **The Lidless.** Ammaret's people (an original race). Six hundred years without night have changed them. Their eyelids withered away generations ago, their eyes are wide, wet and pale gold, and they keep them wet with little brushes on cords. They have never slept.
- **Shadows as currency.** Under a sun that stands straight overhead, a shadow is a small black pool at your feet, and it's the only dark the Lidless have. Shadow-cutters cut shadows off with bone shears, and the Lidless buy them, fold them, and lay them over their open eyes to sleep for a while.
- **The Noon-mask.** The Noonkeepers who went up became Sulmara's Magistrates and forgot everything except the gold mask. Only a Magistrate's hand, in the mask, can open or close the lens.
- **The Blackjackal's plan.** He's a shadow that got loose. In the noon he is small, but in the dark he could be everywhere and wear anyone. He dragged the Magistrate down to close the lens. The Magistrate dropped the mask on the stair on purpose (you found it in Thrumhold), so now the Blackjackal hides in someone in the city and waits for you to bring it.
- **The Dimmers.** Lidless who want the dark so badly they'd give it to him. Led by Brother Lull, they climb the lens tower and close its shutters.

### Systems

- **The Noon (0–10).** It starts at 8 and dims by 1 every bell. At 4 or less it's dim: shadow enemies are hard to hit without a light (attacks at disadvantage). The final boss has 32 + 2 × (10 − Noon) HP. Clearing Nim's market (+1), the Dimmer chapel (+1), arguing Dimmers out of the cult (+1) and opening the lens shutters with the Hour-Key (+3, or +1 by force) brighten it.
- **Your shadow (3 lengths).** You can sell lengths at the Shade Market (12, then 16, then 20 gold) or pay the gate toll or the Dimmers' tithe with one. Oriel Vane sews a length back for 25 gold. With fewer lengths:
  - The Blackjackal's **Wearing** gets easier to suffer: a Wisdom save at DC 9 + 2 per missing length (DC 16 with no shadow).
  - Rest heals half at one length, and not at all at none.
  - **With no shadow at all, Vaelric's axe can't hold you up** ("I hold you by your shadow"). At 0 HP you die, wake in the Temple of the Kept Noon with a stranger's shadow sewn on, lose half your purse, and replay the part from your last rest.
- **Lights.** The Lantern Knight's lamp, the Daylamp, the Sun-Shard and noon-jars (10 gold in the market; smash one for 2d6 radiant, 4d6 against shadows).
- **Time.** Two spans of three bells: First Bell, Second Bell, and the Quiet Bell, when the shade-houses open (3 gold for a cot of real dark, 2d6 HP). Errands in the market take no time.
- **Who is he wearing? (a deduction).** The Blackjackal is wearing one of four people, picked at random each game: Noonkeeper Sabeth Ure, Oriel Vane the shadow-cutter, Master Tollan Rusk of the Hour-House, or Assayer Nim. Each clue clears one innocent: the assayer's scales, the Hour-House, Oriel's errand, the Dimmer chapel, the Magistrate's note under the glass sky, and Tyra in the second span. Three clues and you know. In the House of Shutters you name him. Right: he's torn out early (−8 HP, no first strike). Wrong: he strikes first and the Noon dims.

### Places

- **The Shade Market.** Sell shadow, buy noon-jars. Assayer Nim's scales puzzle (a clue, Noon +1).
- **The Hour-House.** Nine hundred stopped clocks. Master Tollan's dial puzzle earns the Hour-Key and a clue.
- **Oriel Vane's cutting-room.** Catch the loose shade in the drains alive (bring it to 5 HP, then bottle it) for the Shadow Needle, a length back and a clue.
- **The lens galleries.** Iron walkways inside the lighthouse. Fight or sneak past the shutter-Dimmers, then open the shutters.
- **The Dimmers' chapel.** A cistern roofed with sewn shadows. Get in by bluffing, by tithing a length of your shadow, or by arguing with Brother Lull. Inside: the Shutter-Cloak, a clue, and the pole-store to sabotage.
- **The underside of the glass sky.** Climb the old polishing scaffolds, lie under Sulmara's streets, prise out the Sun-Shard, and find the Magistrate's scratched note (a clue) and a rail to the palace roof.
- **The Temple of the Kept Noon.** Sabeth gives you the Lidless Veil. Rest in its light.

### Story beats

- **XLII The Deep Stair.** Thousands of steps. The tongueless bells and three cutshades (fight, run, or ring the bells). Coming out under the glass sky.
- **XLIII Ammaret.** The gate toll (gold, a length of shadow, or show the mask). The Lidless, the shade-sleepers in the streets. Sabeth Ure explains the Kept Noon, the mask and the four suspects. The Dimmers ambush you outside the temple.

## The Undermoon: Kedge (Parts XLIX–LV)

This chapter follows The Kept Noon. Vaelric asked to go home, and the Magistrate's ledger said something under the labyrinth is pushing the roots of the world up. You walk back west to where it all began, and find the labyrinth heaved up out of the sand.

### The Undermoon and the Moored

- **The Undermoon.** A thousand years ago a moon fell. The first wardens caught it in the roots of the great tree and buried it: the great tree was never a tree, it was a cage grown down round a fallen moon, and the labyrinth is the top of the cage. Five iron-wood pins nail the cage to the rock, and the Ember Root sat in the socket at its heart as the lock. The moon is cold and the Ember Root is warm, and while it sat there the moon dreamed of being warm and stayed asleep. Vaelric's old teacher called it the Low Lantern.
- **The Lift.** The Undermoon doesn't pull things down. It lifts them, the way the moon lifts the sea. Since the heist took the Ember Root, it has been waking, and everything near it weighs less: sand falls up in the desert, roots break out of the ground, and in the hollow under the labyrinth loose things float.
- **The Moored** (an original race). The people of Kedge, who have lived beside the Undermoon for a thousand years. Tall, very thin, grey-white and faintly see-through, with silver blood. They're born lighter than air, so they carry their weight: the stones of their dead, braided into their hair or sewn into their clothes, one stone per ancestor, passed down when someone dies. A Moored with no stones floats away. They walk with one hand on a rope, always, and their children wear harnesses clipped to the rails. Their dead with no heirs are kept in the ossuary, a cave of a million named stones.
- **Kedge.** A town hung from the roof of the hollow on a thousand chains, houses like lanterns, walkways of plank and rope, every house weighted with a great stone, directly above the Undermoon.
- **The Unmoored.** Moored who cut off their own stones and float free. They believe their dead live on the moon's face and that when it rises they'll all go home. Led by **Corwen Lisk**, Kedge's tide-reader, they've pulled three of the five pins and mean to pull the last two at the end of the second tide.

### Systems

- **The Lift (0–10).** Starts at 4 and rises by 1 every turn. Driving a pin back lowers it by 2 (by 1 if you drive it crooked), and cutting the Unmoored's tackle in the roost lowers it by 1. Corwen has 28 + 2 × Lift HP.
- **Kinstones and footing.** You need half the Lift (rounded up) in stones to keep your feet. Orrin gives you two guest-stones; the market sells them for 5 gold and buys them back for 3; some things cost a stone (the ossuary's rule, rising after Corwen) and some give one (Tyra's lucky stone, Vaelric's stone). Kite-eels steal them. Your footing is your weight minus what you need:
  - **Adrift** (below 0): disadvantage on attacks (unless clipped to the Tether-Line) and on Strength and Athletics checks. At the end of a turn the Lift may carry you off (Athletics, DC 10 + 2 per stone short): fail and you take 2d4 and pay a line-catcher 5 gold to reel you in.
  - **Footed** (0 to 2): no penalties.
  - **Weighed down** (3 or more, 4 with the Plumb-Heart): disadvantage on Dexterity, Stealth and climbing, and Evade drops from 65% to 40%.
- **Time.** Two tides of three turns: Slack Water, the Flood, and the Ebb, when the knot-houses open (3 gold for a hammock, 2d6 HP). Errands in the market take no time.
- **Moonmilk.** The Moored's silver medicine (12 gold, Mother Kell gives you one). 2d8 + 2 HP, in or out of a fight.
- **Death.** The axe holds you at 1 HP as usual.

### Places

- **The kinstone market.** Buy and sell stones and moonmilk. Ama Pell the stonewright's heir-beam (a balance puzzle) for the Plumb-Heart.
- **The Chainworks.** Gage Tull gives you the Wardens' Maul. Climb down to the moon's face and clear the root-knot choking the North Pin's socket, then drive it.
- **The Drift.** A ring of floating stones circling the moon. Cross it to the Weeping Pin, deal with the two Unmoored on watch (fight or come at them from below), and drive it.
- **The ossuary.** Leave a stone, speak the rite (Wisdom DC 14), or push past (the dead mind: a shelf of stones falls on you). Four Unmoored are hauling up the Grave Pin. If Vaelric still speaks, Sexton Pell shows you the stone the Moored kept for him, and you can carry it.
- **The Lunary.** Corwen's old tide-house. The star-door (Tyra's key no. 44, or Arcana DC 13). Ilo Brack's tide-table puzzle for Corwen's Tide-Chart; persuade him (DC 13, +2 if you read the cords) to learn the weak knot in her cord.
- **The Unmoored roost.** An upside-down chapel on the roof. Float up (hand your stones to Orrin) or climb (Athletics DC 14). Rescue Pim (Stealth or fight), cut their tackle (Lift −1), see their plan. Pim's mother gives you the Tether-Line.
- **Mother Kell's chair.** News, a phial of moonmilk the first time, and rest.

### Puzzles (each has a fallback)

1. **The balance (the drop-line and the heir-beam).** A load hangs on a left hook. Hang stones of weight 1 to 5 on the other hooks so weight × distance balances. Four tests; each says tips left, tips right or level. Fallback: Strength DC 13 (ride it down) or Intelligence DC 14.
2. **The root-knot (the North Socket and the Heart-Knot).** A 6×6 sliding-block puzzle: push roots along their length to get the gold pin out to the right. Four layouts, checked by a solver (13 to 17 pushes), with 12 spare. Fallback: Strength DC 15 / 14.
3. **The Drift.** Rows of floating stones drift left and right with every step and carry you with them. Cross six rows to the far ledge. Each layout is checked by a search for a route of 7 to 14 steps; you get 4 spare. Fallback: Athletics DC 14.
4. **The tide-table.** Eight readings of the Lift follow a repeating pattern of rises and falls. Set the next three. Two tries. Fallback: Wisdom DC 14.
5. **Corwen's dive.** In the boss fight she rises out of reach every second round. Cut a stone loose and rise after her, throw a kinstone, or brace for her dive.

- **XLIX The Road Home.** Parting with the Magistrate. Sand falling up. The Ember Sapling (if you returned the seed) or the Ember Root pulling under your ribs. The labyrinth heaved out of the sand. - **LIV The Tidecaller.** Corwen Lisk: 28 + 2 × Lift HP, AC 14, a flail of her mother's stones (1d10, 2d6 when she dives). ## The Siege of Rahdaria (Parts LVI–LXII)

This chapter follows The Undermoon. Ilo Brack's cords found a second pull from under the western sea. You follow it west to the coast and find Rahdaria, the garden city, under siege.

### The world

- **Rahdaria.** A city of seven stepped terraces rising out of the coast, every terrace a garden (the Hanging Gardens), watered by great wooden screws that lift river water from step to step. Eighty thousand people, fed by the gardens. Ruled by the Council of Terraces under High Steward Nerys Aldane.
- **The Bathyr** (an original race) and their country, **Bathyrra**. The other light that fell a thousand years ago, the Drowned Light, lies on the sea floor and presses down, and round it the sea stands back in a dry bowl. The Bathyr are born too heavy, the Moored turned inside out: short, broad, slate-grey and dense, and they sink in water like stones. They talk by tapping on stone, write in letters pressed into lead, and get giddy on high ground. Since the Undermoon changed, their sky of water has been coming down. They came up out of the sea twelve days ago.
- **Historical siege echoes.** Ration tallies at the bread line, rats sold by the brace, boiled leather and wallpaper paste, the royal menagerie's beasts sold by the pound, letters flown out on kites, hot sand tipped from the walls, a chain across the harbour, the garrison seizing grain, a black market under the menagerie.

### Cast

## The Sounding: Bathyrra (Parts LXIII–LXIX)

The Speaker for the Deep takes the player out past the black fleet, two miles off Rahdaria, and raps a lead plate: a stair rises out of the sea. On its far side the water stands back like a curtain, and the stair runs down the inside of it to **Bathyrra**, the Bathyr city in a dry bowl on the sea floor. Overhead the sea hangs like a sky, held up by the **Drowned Light**, a stone as tall as a tower half sunk in a hill of black glass. It is going out.

*(The **Sea-Sky**, 0–10, starts at 10 (breaking the blockade early earns a little more trust with the Bathyr instead). Errands, wrong answers in the Hall, slipping past the Hushed, and every group fetched in the evacuation bring it down. At 3 or less fights are knee-deep: Evade −15%. Eight words, each a rhythm of short taps (weight 1) and long presses (weight 2): friend ··−, open −·, deep −−, light ·−·, home −··, listen ···−, remember ·−−·, not ··. *(**Forgetting**: the Hushed make you forget fight moves (waking the gems, Evade, catching your breath, binding wounds and so on). In a fight: a Wisdom DC 13 check, or a companion reminds you once. Out of one: a Deafstone, or sounding "remember" at a rest.)*

### Part LXIII: The Sea-Stairs

The Speaker teaches *friend*. The **Sea-Stair**: six landings, four steps wide, some hollow. Hollow counts are pressed into each landing's edge and the top of each column; the player gets five taps to listen first, then steps down one landing at a time onto a step beside the last. ### Part LXIV: Bathyrra

A city of lead roofs and stone doors that ticks like a clock shop, and stops when the player walks by. - *What Keeps the Light*: it slept when it was answered (the dawn-log); it's failing because the Sounders forgot.
- *The Night of Senn*: the order's hush drowned it; the roll of wardens strikes through one name, **Vaelric**.

### Part LXVI: Inside the Axe

A memory, not saved. The player is Vaelric at nineteen in the ring of twelve round Senn, under the Hushwright Edrys Fane. *The hush*: eight breaths, keep the note in the band. ### Part LXVII: The Hushed

## The Black Tithe: the Stillwater Reach (Parts LXX–LXXVI)

The player is caught between both sides: pressed into a pirate crew, they choose whether to rise in it, sell it to the hunters, or break the bargain behind it. Tone: grim and salty.

**The Skerra** (original race): a sea people who live their whole lives afloat and call the land "a grave that's waiting". Tall, narrow, grey-green, with salt crusted white at every joint; black eyes. They can taste a lie on your breath, sweet as rot. Each carries a **count-rope**, a knot for each of their dead, tied in the knot that was that person's. They keep nine **Articles**, cut into the left forearm and rubbed with salt. The ninth: *the drowned you remember can find you, so the sea takes all our dead.*

**The Tithe Fleet**: forty Skerran ships of the Stillwater Reach. Every ship that crosses gives a tenth of its cargo and a tenth of its crew (drawn by knucklebones, one dipped in pitch). Its lord is **Seld Varrick, the Salt King**, genial and generous, who remembers none of his crimes. **The bargain**: thirty years ago he agreed with the Hushwright's people that no ship comes within ten miles of Stillwater; in return the **Quiet Passengers** (Hushed in grey) ride every ship and whisper away the crews' memory of the drowned. He sold them his Fleet's grief, and then they made him forget he had.

**The Saltwrit**: pirate hunters out of Rahdaria under **Commodore Idris Malloway** (flagship *the Patience*, gallows-and-anchor flag). They hang pirates in iron cages on the **Gibbet Skerries**, pressed men with them.

*(State: S.pz. The **Tide-count**, 8 days to the Black Tithe, is the clock; errands, repairs and a failed storm spend it, and days left are an edge in the last fight. **Name in the crew** 0–10 (Pressed, Landsman, Hand, Able hand, Trusted, the crew's choice). **The Saltwrit** 0–10. **Share** under the Articles, paid at Raftmark.)*

*(**Pirate tricks**, kept in S.tricks for every fight after, once a fight each: Belaying pin (1d6 + 2, no roll, half the time it's dazed), Boarding hook (advantage on your next swing, its next blow at disadvantage), Powder flash (2d6 + 2 fire, two of a pack), Salt in the eyes (its next two blows miss), Run up the black (Intimidation: a pack breaks, a lone foe is shaken, −2 to hit).)*

*(**Plunder**, S.plunder, on the roads back: raid caravans (run up the black or fight a small company; take a tenth for +1 or all for +2 and double gold). Tiers: 3 a road-raider (easier to make them give up), 8 a Black Name (+25% gold), 15 Terror of the Reach (running up the black needs no roll). With the Saltwrit's pardon you hunt road-raiders for bounties instead.)*

- **The Saltbones**: **fog** (a sounding puzzle: the chart's soundings are at high water and it's low water now, so the channel reads two fathoms less, and one cast in three is a trap); the derelict **Constant Ann** (put five loose log pages in order by the moon and the water casks, rain included, to find the first chart piece in the ship's bell; then bury her dead or strip her, and a stripped ship is bad luck in the next storm); **wreckers' lights** (three lights, one true: the hand-swung lantern is right for one turn of the light and then isn't).
- **The Lantern Banks**: a fat **Sulmaran merchant** (take a tenth, and the next one pays the tenth without a shot; take it all, and the next one sails with a Saltwrit escort; for a Saltwrit man, rescue her from a lugger); the **floating market** (buy the chart-seller's piece, or win it off Old Lusk at Bones); **Bathyr fishermen** who tap (answer in the Sounding's words and they tell you where Annick Tarrow hides).
- **The Grey Yonder** (after two voyages): a **storm** (the pin-rail, with two lines fouled; a Brineback you spared breaks the sea for you); the **Brineback** (harpoon it, lie still, or feed it the salt pork); a **castaway**: Pell Ashby, if you gave him to the Articles and the rope broke; Malloway in his jolly-boat, if you set him adrift; Wil, if you took his black bone.

### The rival captains

Each fights a broadside duel in her own way. Every round she shows a tell for what she means to do (broadside, rake, board, run or reload), and you answer with **round shot**, **chain** (stops a runner, and stops raking once her rigging's cut), **grape** (thins her crew, and breaks a boarding), **the helm** (slips a rake, halves a broadside), a **double-shotted hold**, **the glass** (shows what she really means) or a **boarding**. Prizes in company add to every broadside.

- **Mother Hessaly Crane**, *the Lamb's Quarter* (the Saltbones): a wrecker. One tell in three is a lie.
- **Bastian Sorrow, called Halfhang**, *the Second Rope* (the Gibbet Skerries): cut down alive off the gibbets. He boards. Carries a chart piece.
- **Annick Tarrow**, *the Glass Eel* (the Lantern Banks): a smuggler who never stands and fights. She runs.
- **Odo Ferrule**, *the Kindly Meant* (the Lantern Banks): a Saltwrit gunnery officer who turned. He rakes.
- **The Brothers Quay**, *the Twinned Fortune* (the Stillwater Reach): two Skerran brothers who still take the tithe (how they got out depends on the ending). Broadside, then board, turn and turn about.
- **Absalom Drear**, *the Long Lament* (the Grey Yonder): a slow ship of the line with twice your guns. Never runs. Carries a chart piece.
- **Commodore Idris Malloway**, *the Patience*, only if you set him adrift and didn't pick him up: he came back for you.

Fates, and what they bring later: **sink** her (her wreck lies on the Saltbones to dive on), take her as a **prize** (she sails with you, up to two), **hang** the captain (from then on nobody strikes to you just because they're short of hands), **maroon** them (they come back, stronger, flying a shirt for a flag), **hand them to the Saltwrit**, or **let them go** (they turn up in a later duel and put a broadside into your enemy, or call off thief-takers on the roads back).

### The Salt King's hoard

The four pieces of the chart (the *Constant Ann*'s bell, Old Lusk at the market, Halfhang's coat, Drear's cabin) open **Knucklebone Key** in the Grey Yonder. Lay the chart flat (four pieces, edges matched, open sea all round), fight the six old hoard-keepers in the cave, and open the **knot-lock**: three knotted ropes, overhand one, figure-eight five, double ten, and the dial wants the sea's tenth of the total. Without Varrick's knot-count you work the values out from two reckonings cut by the door. Then the choice, which Raftmark and the end card remember: give it back to the families, keep it (a fortune, and a bigger price on you), give it to the Saltwrit (your name off every list), or give it to the sea and keep only the Tithe-Cup (+4 max HP).

### On the roads back

A **price on your head** (pirates) or a **pirates' grudge** (a Saltwrit man) brings thief-takers or a grudge-crew now and then. Pay them off, talk them down, name a captain who owes you, or fight (and more come). Raiders with a name can take a **Saltwrit salt-tax wagon** instead of a caravan. Raftmark's townsfolk talk about the hoard, the bell, the hanging-book, the lights, your prizes, your marooned captains and your mutiny.

## The Deafstones: lore tablets

A fallen light wakes to loud voices, so the first wardens, the Whisperers (founded by Ilka Moth, a Greenreach shepherd the Hesk taught to hear), kept the Undermoon asleep by whispering, and whispered the lands' memories away while they were at it. Vaelric was the last of them, and the one who started to doubt. Stone, lead and gold can't hear, so the people they hushed carved the truth into them.

*(Three tablets per region: the Root, the Hush and the Wound. Two wait at the end of the part named; the third, and any missed, only on the roads back. Finding one is a check (the DC given), then a charcoal rubbing: rub every square and avoid the cracks. 0 smudges is a clean rubbing; 1–2 lose a few words; more lose many. Words in a script the player hasn't learned stay blank until they have. Afterwards the player leaves it standing (Hear) or pries it up for 1d6+4 gold, breaking it (Hush). All three of a region give +1 on non-attack checks there. All thirty open Ilka's Stone.)*

*(The Hear or Hush balance counts those choices plus: shielding Sable's crew (Hear) or not (Hush); talking Tobbin down (Hear); returning the Ember Root (Hear 2) or keeping it (Hush); the Hesk truce (Hear 2), singing them to sleep (Hush) or the blast (Hush 2); a dusk for Ammaret (Hear 2), night (Hear) or keeping the noon (Hush); letting the Undermoon rise (Hear) or caging it again either way (Hush).)*

### The Labyrinth

- **The Root: The Tree Grown Down** (stone; after Part III, The Hollow Chamber; Investigation DC 11). Behind the split trunk, where the roots knot together, one root has grown right round a flat grey stone, and the stone has letters on it. *"We did not plant this tree. We grew it down. A light came out of the sky in the year of no harvest, and it was a moon, and it was still falling when it struck. Ilka said: it is only sleeping if we are quiet. So we were quiet, and we grew the tree round it, root over root, a mile deep. This chamber is the knot at the top."*
- **The Hush: What the Library Keeps** (lead, Moored knot-cords; after Part V, The Library of Roots; Perception DC 12). One shelf in the Library holds no books at all, only a lead plate wedged at the back, with a cord of tiny knotted stones nailed through it. *"This is not a library. It is where the wardens bring what they take. Every book on these shelves was a memory once, and it was somebody's. My grandmother's name is in one of them. I have tied it into these knots so that it stays. Read nothing here. Ask whose it was."* (If Vaelric still speaks:) "Knowledge is the only treasure that grows," Vaelric murmurs. "We used to say that. We never said whose."
- **The Wound: A Finger Closer** (stone; roads back; Investigation DC 13). At the labyrinth's outer gate, the wind has worn the spirals and antlered figures down far enough to show an older carving underneath them. *"Whoever reads this: the wardens are gone, and the knot is loosening. What they made the world forget is still under your feet, and every year the roots come up a finger closer. If a soft voice ever tells you that you are safe as long as you are quiet, do not let it whisper you. Ask it what it buried."*

### The Burning Sands

- **The Root: The Greenreach** (stone; after Part VII, The Burning Sands; Investigation DC 12). The storm has scoured the buried arch clean. Under its keystone, letters are cut deep into the sandstone, as sharp as the day they were made. *"Here was the Greenreach: grass to a horse's chest, from this arch to the sea. The wardens came with a seed in a box and a song in their mouths, and the seed drank. It drank the warm out of the ground, out of the rain, out of us. They said a cold moon needs a warm lock. We called it the Ember."*
- **The Hush: Gold Remembers** (gold; after Part X, The Ember Root; Perception DC 12). In the Ember Root's hollow, a thin plate of beaten gold has been pressed into the wall at knee height, where a child would see it. *"They asked us to forget the grass, and we did. My son was born in sand and has never seen green. When I tell him about it he thinks I am telling him a story. Gold remembers what it is told. I have pressed this into gold so the story outlives the telling."*
- **The Wound: Nine Arches** (stone; roads back; Survival DC 13). Out where the Cascades have stripped a dune, a row of arch-tops pokes out of the sand in a dead-straight line, and the last of them is carved. *"Count the arches. There were nine waystations between the Reach and the knot. At every one the wardens slept, and whispered, and went on, and behind them the grass went brown. The desert is not a desert. It is a road, and it leads to the lock."*

### The Hushfen

- **The Root: The Kingdom of Senn** (stone; after Part XIII, The Violet Baron's Lair; Investigation DC 13). In the Violet Baron's trophy hall, one of the trophies is a slab of grey stone, hung upside down so that nobody would bother to read it. *"Senn was a kingdom of reed and silver. Its queen found a stone like this one in a ploughed field, and read it, and began to remember. Within a year all of Senn was remembering, out loud, in the streets."*
- **The Hush: Forty at Dusk** (stone; after Part XV, The Glade; Perception DC 12). At the heart of the Glade a stone stands in the clear water, and the moss grows on every side of it but the face. *"The wardens came at dusk, forty of them, and stood round Senn in a ring and whispered all night. In the morning nobody in Senn knew their own name. By the next spring the ground had forgotten how to hold us up. We are not dead. We are waiting for somebody to say us."* (If Vaelric still speaks:) Vaelric is quiet for a long time. "I was there," he says at last. "I was young. They gave me a torch to hold."
- **The Wound: The Names of Senn** (lead; roads back; Investigation DC 13). The Hushfen has sunk another foot. Where a causeway used to be, a lead sheet has come up out of the mud, covered edge to edge in small careful letters. *"These are the names of Senn, as many as we could keep: Aske the reed-cutter, Morrow, Pell and her six daughters, old Iddy who kept the bees, Coyne, Tamsy, the twins from the mill, and Queen Ressa, who read the stone. Say one aloud, and somewhere in the mire something turns over in its sleep."*

### Carrowmere

- **The Root: The Ledger of the Hushed** (stone; after Part XIX, The Counting House; Investigation DC 12). In the counting house, the oldest desk has a stone top, and the ledger-lines ruled into it are not for money. *"Carrowmere was built to count. Not coin: the hushed. Every name the wardens took was written here in a ledger nobody was allowed to read, so that one day it could all be given back. The Harrowgates kept the ledger. In time the Harrowgates forgot why."*
- **The Hush: The Lantern's Promise** (lead, Bathyr pressed lead; after Part XXII, The Drowned Treasury; Perception DC 13). Under a floor-slab the flood has lifted in the treasury, there's a lead tablet stamped with rows of square dents, and a few lines in the common hand. *"The lantern was lit from below. A trader of the deep people came up the river with a sliver of their light in a lead box, and gave it to the first Harrowgate for a promise. The promise was to remember them. It was the first thing to go out of the ledger."* (If Vaelric still speaks:) "Oswin's lamp," Vaelric breathes. "It never goes out. Now I know why."
- **The Wound: Under the Lake** (stone; roads back; Investigation DC 14). The water running up the treasury walls has bared a stone set into the very foot of the keep, carved in the same square hand as the ledger. *"The king opened the floodgates to save a barge of coin, and the ledger went down with the castle. Every name in it is under this lake. If the lake is ever drained, the hushed of a hundred years will have their names back in a single night, and there will be a great deal of weeping."*

### Lastwell

- **The Root: Ninety Feet** (stone; after Part XXIV, The Well and the Bell; Investigation DC 12). One of the well's capstones has been turned face-down. Somebody has scratched letters into its underside, where only a person lying in the mud would see them. *"Maud dug the well. Forty feet and dry, sixty and dry, and at ninety the water came up cold enough to crack a cup. It came out of a root as thick as a church. The water tasted of nothing. Nobody should drink water that tastes of nothing."*
- **The Hush: The Warden's Prayer** (stone; after Part XXVI, The Belfry; Perception DC 13). In the churchyard, the one grave with no puddle on it has a second stone laid flat at its foot, carved with four words and a great deal more. *"Bell. Root. Hand. Eye. Hear the bell, mind the root, still the hand, close the eye. That is the wardens' prayer, and it is a lie. The digger said it every night, and drank anyway. What he drank was the moon's cold, and it made him thirsty for ever."*
- **The Wound: The One Who Said No** (stone; roads back; Investigation DC 13). With the bell down out of the Belfry, its old bed-stone shows a carving on its underside, done by somebody in a hurry. *"The wardens came to Lastwell after. They meant to burn it, to keep the thing in the well a secret. One of them said no. They argued in the square all night, and in the morning they went away and left us with it. I don't know which was kinder. The one who said no carried an axe with three stones in the haft."* (If Vaelric still speaks:) "I should have stayed," Vaelric says. "I told myself not burning it was enough."

### Sulmara

- **The Root: Up Through the Glass** (gold; after Part XXX, The First Day; Investigation DC 12). In a niche of the old Magistrates' stair, half hidden by a tapestry, a gold tablet hangs where nobody climbing would think to look. *"We came up through the glass with our eyes burned and our minds empty. The wardens met us at the top with water and soft voices. When they were done we were the Magistrates of a new city, and we could not remember the old one. Only the mask remembered. Gold cannot hear."*
- **The Hush: The Law of Clear Sight** (gold; after Part XXXIII, The Glass Assize; Insight DC 13). Behind the Glass Judge's bench, under the varnish, the panel of the wall is gold, and a law is written on it in a careful old hand. *"I, the fourth Magistrate, write this in my own hand on gold: in my court no one shall be made to forget. What is seen is said. I do not know why I feel this so hard. Some nights I wake with somebody else's name in my mouth."*
- **The Wound: Their Sky** (stone; roads back; Perception DC 13). The vault's witness glass is all showing you, but one pane, low down, shows writing instead: a stone tablet on the far side of the glass, in Ammaret, facing up. *"Under every street of Sulmara there is a street of Ammaret, and the soles of our feet are their sky. We walk on the faces of our grandparents and call it glass."*

### Mount Corrach

- **The Root: Before Words** (stone, Hesk song-script; after Part XXXVI, The First Day under Corrach; Perception DC 12). On the trail up from Ashgate, a stone shelf has been carved into the cliff in long wavering lines, like the trace of a song. *"Before the people of the flat lands had words, we sang. Our full song shapes the soft things of the world: fungus, shell and salt. Sung loud enough, it shapes the hard things too. The light under the tree heard us once, and turned over in its sleep."*
- **The Hush: The Shepherd's Summer** (stone, Hesk song-script; after Part XL, Thrumhold; Investigation DC 13). In Thrumhold's oldest hall, the wall behind the empty cradles is cut with the same wavering lines, much worn, as if a great many hands had touched it. *"The shepherd Ilka came up the mountain alone and sat with us a whole summer. We taught her to hear. She asked us to sleep seventeen years and sing for one summer, so the light would not wake. We agreed. We did not know she would take our quiet song down the mountain and use it on people."*
- **The Wound: The Unfinished Song** (stone, Hesk song-script; roads back; Investigation DC 13). Beside a ring of Bloom that won't grow back, a song-stone stands, its lines cut off short as if the carver stopped mid-breath. *"The Bloom is an unfinished song. It grows wherever a singer stopped before the end. The Long Drummer woke early and sick, and every song in Corrach was cut off in the middle. Finish them, and the Bloom will go back to sleep with us."*

### Ammaret

- **The Root: The Soft-Voiced Ones** (stone, Lidless shadow-script; after Part XLIII, Ammaret; Investigation DC 12). In a shade-seller's stall, the slab they cut shadows on is an old carved stone, its letters filled in with folded scraps of dark. *"The soft-voiced ones came down from the north and told us: the dark wakes the moon. Keep the dark away and you will be safe. We believed them. So we built the lens, and caught the last noon in it."*
- **The Hush: Sealed in Light** (stone, Lidless shadow-script; after Part XLVI, The House of Shutters; Perception DC 13). At the foot of the lens tower, where the shadow of the tower should be and isn't, there's a stone laid into the paving, carved in two hands. *"On the day of the last noon the lens drew the sun down too hard, and above us the sand ran like honey and set like glass. We were sealed in our own light. Our eyelids were the first thing we lost. Our night was the second. The truth was the third."*
- **The Wound: A Sentence** (stone, Lidless shadow-script; roads back; Insight DC 13). In the House of Shutters, the Cascade has knocked a slat loose, and behind it, in the only shadow in Ammaret that's never been cut, a small stone is waiting. *"There was never any danger in our dark. The moon is a thousand miles of rock away from here. Six hundred years without sleep, for a sentence a stranger whispered."*

### The Undermoon

- **The Root: On the Hill** (stone, Moored knot-cords; after Part L, Kedge; Investigation DC 12). A guest-stone on the line down to Kedge is bigger than the rest, and flat, and somebody has carved its face and tied a knot-cord through a hole in it. *"We were the Greenreach herders, standing on the hill when it fell. The Lift took hold of us and has never let go. Our children were born light. We carry our dead so we will not float away."*
- **The Hush: You Cannot Whisper a Grandmother** (stone, Moored knot-cords; after Part LIII, The Heartfall; Perception DC 13). In the ossuary, among a million named stones, one is carved on both sides, and its knot-cord is older than the cave's. *"The wardens tried to hush us like everyone else. It would not take. A Moored wears her grandmother on a cord round her neck, and you cannot whisper somebody out of a memory they are wearing. So the wardens left us alone, and this ossuary is the only true record of the world."*
- **The Wound: A Million Stones** (stone, Moored knot-cords; roads back; Investigation DC 13). Under Kedge, the swinging chains have scraped the cave wall bare, and where the rock shows through, there are knots cut into it, row on row. *"A million named stones, and every one a life the hush could not touch. If anyone ever wants to know what really happened, they will have to come here and read us, one by one."*

### Rahdaria

- **The Root: Two Peoples** (lead, Bathyr pressed lead; after Part LVII, The First Days; Investigation DC 13). At the foot of the great water-screw on the lowest terrace, the bearing-block is a slab of lead, and it's stamped all over with square dents under a line of plain words. *"Rahdaria was founded by two peoples. The heavy ones came up out of the sea and the light ones came down off the grass, and they built the terraces together. The screws that lift the water are ours. We cut them from the drowned forests."*
- **The Hush: The Sea-Stair** (lead, Bathyr pressed lead; after Part LXII, The Gardens of Rahdaria; Perception DC 13). In the Keeper's sheds above the Hanging Gardens, the old harvest scale has a lead counterweight, and the weight is a tablet. *"The gardens were planted to feed the keepers of the Drowned Light. Every harvest went down the sea-stair in carts. Then the soft-voiced ones came, and the city forgot the stair, and the deep people forgot the city. We left this in lead, which does not listen."* (If Vaelric still speaks:) "Not a conquest," Vaelric says softly. "A homecoming nobody remembered. We did that."
- **The Wound: Half a City** (lead, Bathyr pressed lead; roads back; Investigation DC 14). On the floor of the dry Lily cistern, beside the place the Inquirer's box lay, a second lead box has come to light, and inside it is a single tablet. *"The heavy ones did not come up to conquer. They came home, and nobody knew them. If the light under the sea goes out, the sea will come up the terraces, and Rahdaria will drown with its other half."*

### Ilka's Stone

*(Once every Deafstone is found.)* *"My name is Ilka Moth, and I was a shepherd. I heard the moon coming three days before it fell. The Hesk taught me how to make a frightened thing sleep, and I did it, and it worked. Then I did it to a frightened village. Then to a curious one. Then to one that was only asking. I carved this after, on the island, where nobody can hear me. Whoever has found every stone: now you know what I did. Do better than I did. Listen first."*
