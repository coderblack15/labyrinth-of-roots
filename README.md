# Labyrinth of Roots

**Created by: Adrian Blackshire and Claude**

## Introduction

Far below an old ceremonial road lies a labyrinth grown from living roots, carved with druidic runes and guarded long ago by a warden named Vaelric. Travelers say nothing that goes down there comes back up. You are a lone adventurer with a pack, a belt knife and a flint, and you are going down anyway.

Labyrinth of Roots is a choose-and-roll dark fantasy adventure in seventy-six parts. Walk the ceremonial pathway, descend past the druid gate, take up Vaelric's gem-set axe, and face Ryytaa the Ender in her nest before climbing out into the desert, where a crew of thieves called the Hollow Hands wants your axe for one impossible heist, and on into the Hushfen, a drowned swamp ruled by a robber baron in violet, and out across its lake to Carrowmere, the drowned castle, where a knight with a lantern has guarded a stolen treasury for a hundred years, and down the road to Lastwell, a dead village where something you can't see drinks people dry, and hunts you while you scrape together water, food, rune mines and caltrops to survive it, and back to Sulmara, the Glass City, where you stand trial before the Glass Judge with a rogue named Tyra Quillon at your side, and finally north to the valley under Mount Corrach, where the Hesk, a tall, shelled people who sleep seventeen years at a time, have woken early, carrying a fungus called the Pale Bloom that Vaelric's axe can't save you from, and then down the deep stair under the mountain to Ammaret, the buried city under Sulmara, where it has been noon for six hundred years, the Lidless sell their shadows to sleep, and the Blackjackal is hiding inside one of four people, and at last back home to the labyrinth, which has heaved up out of the sand, and down past the bottom of it to Kedge, a town hung on chains over a moon that fell a thousand years ago and is waking up, and then west to the sea, to Rahdaria, a city of hanging gardens besieged by a people who came up out of the bottom of it, where you turn detective for a starving city and then break the blockade, and at last down the Sea-Stair into Bathyrra, the dry city under the western sea, where the sky is made of water and the light that holds it up is going out, and then out across the Stillwater Reach, where the Skerran raiders of the Tithe Fleet take a tenth of every ship's cargo and a tenth of its crew, and the tenth is you.

## How to play

1. Read each scene, then pick what you do next.
2. When a check comes up, click to roll the d20. Your bonus is added, and you need to meet or beat the number shown.
3. Keep an eye on your character sheet. It tracks your HP, armor class, gold and everything you find.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in any browser. It's a single self-contained page with no build step.

To play it online, turn on GitHub Pages: **Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → `main` / root**. The game will then be live at `https://coderblack15.github.io/labyrinth-of-roots/`.

## Free demo

`demo.html` is a free demo that plays Parts I–X and then shows an end card pointing to the full game. It's generated from `index.html`, so rebuild it after any change to the game:

```
python3 tools/build-demo.py --parts 10 --full-url https://coderblack15.itch.io/labyrinth-of-roots
```

`TIP_URL` (the Support the creator link) and `FULL_URL` (where the demo's "Play the full game" button goes) are set near the top of the script in `index.html`.

## Playtesting

`tools/playtest.js` plays the game by itself in a headless browser: it reads each scene, picks choices (preferring ones it hasn't tried), rolls the dice and pokes at the puzzles, and stops with a report if the page throws a script error, leaves nothing to press (a soft-lock), or freezes (it records where). It saves the game at the start of every part, so one chapter can be replayed from its first part. It needs Playwright (`npm i playwright`).

```
node tools/playtest.js --seed 1                      # a whole run (about 5,000-10,000 clicks)
node tools/playtest.js --seed 2 --from playtest-1/save-part24.json --stop 28   # just Lastwell's end
node tools/playtest.js --seed 3 --from playtest-1/save-part24.json --prefer "Climb the bell rope|Leave the rafters"
```

Run a few seeds after any change, and after adding a chapter.

## What's in the game

- **A WARNING screen** that greets every visitor. Press *ENTER* to go in.
- **A landing page** that sets the mood before you play. *Enter the Labyrinth* and *Play Now* take you straight to the start of the game.
- **Choices** at key moments, such as paying the hooded strangers, fighting or fleeing the vine horror, and bracing your shield against the spiders.
- **Clickable d20 rolls** for every check: Perception, Survival, Dexterity checks, Investigation (DC 12), the Dexterity save (DC 13), attack rolls, and the Shield Slam Strength contest against Ryytaa. Success and failure each lead to their own text.
- **An enemy health meter** on the character sheet in every fight, from Ryytaa the Ender to the Wringer. Mob fights (guards, slugs) count heads instead of HP.
- **Combat combos**: each hit you land in a row adds +1 to your next attack (up to +2), the second hit deals +2 damage, and the third is a **Rootbreaker** that doubles your damage dice (or cuts through into a second foe in a mob fight) and spends the combo. A miss breaks the chain. Spells and gem bursts that land keep it alive.
- **A character sheet** that tracks HP, armor class, gold, and inventory: the torch, Vaelric's axe (its gem power works once per encounter), the pauldron shield, and the spellbooks you choose in the Library of Roots.
- **Survival in Lastwell** (Parts XXIII–XXVIII): water, food, five levels of exhaustion, ooze sickness, and the Hunt clock that brings the unseen Wringer down on you when you make noise.
- **Snare rune mines** (fire, poison and frost), each placed with an Arcana roll, that combine and that the Wringer learns to shrug off. You can scribe more scrolls with ooze ink, and forge **caltrops** from scrap iron once you find Hobb's book.
- **Four puzzles**: the rune-sealed cellar, scribing a rune from memory, crossing the rotten rafters of the spire, and the well-keeper's four-ring lock.
- **A recovery phase** in the belfry, where a Scroll of Revelation shows you the well-keeper's secret food stash.
- **Healing** in Lastwell: Draughts of Mending (+10 HP), Elixirs of Health (+20 HP), and gravemoss poultices from the old graves.
- **The Glass Assize** (Parts XXIX–XXXIV): two days in Sulmara with a Heat clock, disguises, the Vitreous Library's skill books (Lockcraft, Jurisprudence, Cardsharp, Streetwise, and the crafting skills Glasswright and Herbcraft), the Cracked Lantern inn, the Slag & Siphon bar (drinking contest and the High Glass card game), escort missions, a trial before a twelve-juror jury, and the sun-mirror at noon.
- **Your own name**: name your adventurer when you begin (each save slot keeps its own), and Sable, the clerks, the Glass Judge's warrant and others will use it.
- **Your company**: Burr, a dog who follows you out of the labyrinth, and Tyra Quillon are friends with a bond of 0 to 5 that grows with every third fight won together and an evening at camp after each chapter. Each step unlocks more help: a bite or a knife in fights, a blocked blow, a bonus on checks. Three sellswords (Odran Vey's shield, Wenna Strake's crossbow and Mother Caddock's stitching) can be hired at the start of most chapters, one at a time, and one who serves three chapters or camp evenings swears to you and travels for free. Companions soften foes up and catch blows, but the killing blow is always yours. The Hushfen lets one walker in, so your company waits outside it.
- **Tyra Quillon**, a laid-back rogue companion who runs errands, collects keys and cats, does odd jobs with you, and might fall for you.
- **More puzzles**: rebuilding a shattered witness pane, steering sunlight through mirror rigs, breaking a two-dial cipher, and cross-examining a lying witness.
- **The Corrach Three** (Parts XXXV–XLI): three days in the mining town of Ashgate, then the Singing Door, a snaking tunnel, and Thrumhold, the Hesk fortress in a 200-foot cavern of carved stone homes.
- **The Pale Bloom**, a disease that stops Vaelric's axe from keeping you on your feet. While you carry it, 0 HP means death: you wake at Vellum Rest, lose half your purse, and replay the part. Saltroot tea, firebaths, a saltroot mask and moltwater fight it.
- **Staying alive** in the Hesk fights: **Evade** gives up your swing for a 65% chance to slip every attack that round, and Sister Wren teaches **Field Mending** (first aid, 10 gold, also sold at the Vitreous Library) to bind your wounds twice a fight and patch up after every win.
- **Quick hands**: in any fight, every second turn you can use an item on top of your move (the Use an item button on the fight prompt, or Use on the item in your pack). Every use is a timing catch: stop the marker in the gold for a clean use, on the bright notch for a perfect one (half again as strong, and ready a turn sooner), or fumble it for half. Wait past your two turns and the notch gets wider. Healing is a quick gulp mid-fight (8 HP from a Draught, 15 from an Elixir); other items blind foes (the lens, a fistful of cullet), break a Hesk blade-song (a shatter flask), push back the Bloom (saltroot tea) or ward off the next Bloom save (raw saltroot).
- **Spoils**: every fight you come through leaves a leaf of a Scroll of Acquisition; four leaves bind into a scroll that teaches you a skill of your choice. Every foe you put down leaves salvage (cloth scraps, scrap metal, and Bloom spore sacks from the Hesk) that a tinker buys at the start of the next part.
- **Trapcraft**: schematics turn up in the spoils of fights (the first one teaches you the skill; so does Hobb's book, the Scroll of Acquisition, or *A Trapper's Hundred Knots* in the Vitreous Library). Once you know it, you keep your salvage metal and cloth for parts. **Small** traps (Jawbite, Tanglewire) are thrown mid-fight with quick hands; **medium** traps (Swing-Log, Stake Pit) are set before a road fight; **huge** twelve-foot traps (the Gallows Drop, the Thresher Wheel) are raised at build sites a chapter offers, against a great foe or a column of troops. Building is a rig puzzle (study the schematic, fit the parts in order), and a design you've built twice you know by heart.
- **Innovation**: you earn *insights* the hard way, from springing traps, bringing things down, failed builds, and your first fight in each kind of weather. Spend them at your trapper's kit to combine two schematics into a design of your own (Bramble Jaw, Pendulum Pit, the Rootcage) or to learn Load Paths, Weather Eye or Quick Set.
- **Weather and tremors**: roads roll rain, fog, high wind, thunderstorms, blazing heat or snow by region, each with one plain effect on fights, and you can see it fall. Underground roads can shake instead: the ground heaves mid-fight, and loose stone comes down more easily.
- **The ground underfoot**: terrain hazards in their home countries, about one road fight in eight there (never two running, never a boss): **sinking sand** in the Burning Sands (lure a foe in and the sand takes it, spoils and all, or blunder in and haul yourself out before it reaches your chest), **landslides** on Mount Corrach (a warning round: run, get behind a pavise, or bring the slope down on them; it blocks the salt road to Ashgate until the town, the navvies or you dig it out) and **earthquakes** under the labyrinth (everyone knocked down, packs scattered, a crack to shove a foe into or reach down for a cache; the crack stays open on the roads back). Quakes come one fight in five once the Undermoon has risen. Three set scenes (Jessamy Tarn's sinking wagon, the salt train under the Hanging Scree, the Rooks when the floor gives) are remembered in the den, in Ashgate and in the Library of Roots. Five pursuits in the set The Ground Underfoot.
- **Sabotage**: some roads have something worth bringing down (a dead elm, a kiln chimney, a hanging tooth of moonstone). Hunt for its keystone (each wrong stone creaks out how far away the keystone is), then let it go when your foe is under it. The **Undermining** skill makes it easier.
- **The Reckoning Board**: bounties paid dead or alive (Wat Gorse, Corvin Dray the Thrice-Seen, the raid-leader Skarrit, a quiet assassination contract), with ranks from Copper to Silver.
- **The Coffer-House**, Ashgate's bank: 15 gold to open an account, and banked gold survives a death.
- **The Hesk song-language**, learned in lessons with the exile Ninefold: each level opens new ways to talk, sneak and fight.
- **Eight quest-earned items**: the Weathercloak (fire, frost and poison threads), Mirrorwake (a blade that splits you into copies), the Drum of Stilling, the Saltroot Mask, the Daylamp, the Long Reckoning crossbow, the Seventeen-Year Ring, and the Moltglaive.
- **Treasure hunts**: three riddle maps to dig up, and more puzzles: a tone-lock door, a fog-shrouded tunnel maze, shoring a collapsed mine, a shell game against an illusionist, and singing a drum-call back to break it.
- **The Kept Noon** (Parts XLII–XLVIII): down the deep stair to Ammaret, the buried city under Sulmara's glass, where the Lidless have had no night for six hundred years. Sell your shadow (and risk the axe's protection), keep the Noon from dimming, find out which of four people the Blackjackal is wearing, and decide whether the city keeps its light.
- **More puzzles**: the Hour-House's linked clock dials, the assayer's scales (find the false shadow in two weighings), two shadow gates (set shutter-stones so the moon glyphs fall in shadow and the sun glyphs stay lit), and pinning the Blackjackal's shadow on the wall.
- **Five more quest items**: the Sun-Shard, the Hour-Key, Oriel's Shadow Needle, the Lidless Veil and the Dimmer's Shutter-Cloak.
- **The Undermoon** (Parts XLIX–LV): home to the labyrinth, and under it to Kedge, where the Moored carry the stones of their dead so they don't float away. The Lift rises every turn: carry enough kinstones to keep your feet, but not so many you can't climb. Drive the wardens' pins back into the cage, stop Corwen Lisk the Tidecaller, who fights from the air, and decide whether the fallen moon sleeps or rises.
- **More puzzles**: balance beams (weight times distance), a sliding root-knot, the Drift (stepping stones that move every time you do), and the tide-readers' tide-table.
- **More quest items**: the Plumb-Heart, the Wardens' Maul, the Tether-Line, Corwen's Tide-Chart and the Ember Sapling.
- **The Siege of Rahdaria** (Parts LVI–LXII): west to the coast, where the garden city of Rahdaria is besieged by the Bathyr, a people who came up out of the bottom of the sea. The High Steward makes you her Inquirer: a **Detective** ability (the Inquirer's Eye) and a casebook of **seven cases**, each with three clues to find in three days of four watches. The city's **Hunger** rises every dawn; feed it and end the blight in the poisoned Hanging Gardens. Your **Standing** with the people gives up to 20% off at the inn, the smithy and the stalls. Then the Bathyr seal the city: break the **blockade** (a hundred soldiers, a hundred crates, three Generals) with sorties, raids and one **sabotage mission** in three branches (the Flood, the Fire, the Fall) that you plan from what you've found. An achievement for completing every objective. Under the cases run the **Seven Inquiries**: red herrings, suspects on the Inner Council, the Rat King's five faces, the spies Whisper, Rook and Veil, Inspector Amara Holt challenging every accusation, and the Curator under it all. With them come character backgrounds, **Detective Vision** (a trace-following puzzle), a **War Journal**, five Rahdarian allies (party of two), three paths through the blockade (military, sabotage, diplomacy), six battlefield professions with their own mini-games, a volley every 12 turns, retreat and enemy momentum (at 100%, Rahdaria falls), Adaptive Reasoning ranks II–IV, the Defender of Rahdaria tiers, and the grand feat, **The Eye of Rahdaria**.
- **More puzzles**: tracing the poison through the cisterns, auditing a grain ledger, a watch-rota logic grid, a knock code, mixing soil amendments, and reading Bathyr lead-letters on a cipher wheel.
- **The Sounding** (Parts LXIII–LXIX): down the Sea-Stair with the Speaker for the Deep to **Bathyrra**, the Bathyr city in a dry bowl under the western sea, where the Drowned Light holds the sea up like a sky. The **Sea-Sky** (0–10) is the chapter's clock: errands, wrong answers and slow work bring it down, and at 3 or less you fight knee-deep. Learn **Sounding**, the Bathyr tap language (eight words, each a rhythm of taps and presses you feel once and tap back), and use it to open the lead doors of the **Hall of Lead**, where a phrase has to weigh what the door weighs. Read the **Two Truths** (a Hushkeepers' gold tablet, a Bathyr lead one and the witness the Hall kept) to learn how Rahdaria was really founded, what kept the Light asleep, and who drowned Senn. Play a memory **Inside the Axe** as young Vaelric on the Night of Senn. The **Hushed**, the last of the Hushkeepers' order, make you **forget** fight moves until a friend, a Deafstone or the word *remember* brings them back; argue their five down with what you've learned, slip past, or fight, then face **Severine Gall, the Hushwright**. Then the sky comes down: choose who to get to the stair, and what's left is all the time you'll have at the Light. Three endings: **Sound it** (the Bathyr's note, the first of a Listener's five), **let it be hushed**, or **let Vaelric sing it**. Two new road creatures (the Wall-gar and the Quietings), three Deafstones, a relic, a town and a sanctuary for the roads back, the Plummet mine, and ten pursuits in a new set.
- **More puzzles**: the Sea-Stair (find the hollow steps from the counts pressed into the landings, with a few taps to listen first), Sounding (rhythm memory), lead doors (weight sums with rules), the Two Truths, and the hush (keep young Vaelric's breath in the band).
- **The Black Tithe** (Parts LXX–LXXVI): sailing for Stillwater, your cog is taken by the **Tithe Fleet**, raiders of the Stillwater Reach, and you're the tenth of the crew the sea takes. The **Skerra** are an original sea people who never stand on land, crusted with salt at every joint, who can taste a lie on your breath and keep a **count-rope** with a knot for each of their dead. Sign the **Articles** in your own arm or be keelhauled. Aboard the *Widow's Tally* the **Tide-count** (8 days to the Black Tithe at Raftmark) is the clock, your **name in the crew** (Pressed to the crew's choice) decides what they'll let you do, and the **Saltwrit**, the pirate hunters out of Rahdaria, would like a friend aboard. Five errands, each a puzzle and a crew-mate to win: the pin-rail (which belaying pin works which sails), the powder room (find the damp cartridge in two weighings), **Bones** in the fo'c'sle (a bluffing dice game), the harpoon watch (a brackray over the rail), and the shares (divide a prize by the Articles). Then a prize chase through the Teeth (steer a grid of rocks with the tide setting you), the bow-chaser, a boarding battle, the tithe of the prize's crew, a skimmer to name by the dawn bell (one liar among four), and a storm. At **Raftmark**, a town of lashed hulks, the Salt King has sold his Fleet's grief to the Hushed of Stillwater. Three ways to end it: **rise** to captain (a vote under the Articles or a duel with Orla Tennick, then a broadside and boarding the Saltwrit flagship), **sell** the Fleet to the Saltwrit (work out their flag code, then storm the Salt King's hulk), or **burn** the bargain (retie the count-ropes so the Fleet remembers its dead). **Pirate tricks**, learned from the crew and kept for every fight after: the belaying pin, the boarding hook, a powder flash, salt in the eyes, and running up the black. **Plunder** on the roads back: raid caravans (take a tenth or take it all), or, with the Saltwrit's pardon, hunt road-raiders for bounties; at 3, 8 and 15 Plunder the raids get easier and richer. Two new road creatures (the Brackray and the Wreck-gulls), three Deafstones, a relic, a town and a sanctuary, the Smoke-pot mine, and thirteen pursuits in a new set.
- **Half the East** (Parts LXXVII–LXXXIII): at Stillwater a Cascade rises out of the still water, and when you cut it down one silver thread ties itself into a message in your palm (read the knots in order of their turns): Ostle Ford, east of Rahdaria, and a sleeper named **Ysolt**, a mapmaker who walked into a Cascade eleven years ago and has drawn what it shows her ever since. At Ostle Ford, **Mardek Corvane, the Surveyor**, drinks the dying miller's soul in front of you (the first vision of the dead), cuts you once, learns your stance, and takes half of Ysolt's map (or a little of Tyra). Find what the miller hid from Ysolt's sleep-talk in bearings, join her nine sheets (turn them till every road and river runs on), and read the plot: **the Hinge**, the houses the realm turns on (the granaries of Rahdaria, the glass of Sulmara, the Saltwrit's purse), mean to hang Bathyr-lead bells on five towers and ring the Leaning from the **Gnomon**, so every Cascade leans east and the land between goes into the dream, and the Bathyr get the blame. **The Blank** (leagues of the east still drawn on her map) is the clock. At the Hinge's masked dinner at Gauntry House, match five members to five masks from what you overhear, steal the map back, and fight **Idony Rusk**, the Hinge's Knife, in a **Fury bout**: no turns, attack without stopping, Fury fills on every stroke that lands, they strike only when you hesitate, loop to catch a wind-up, and Unleash a frenzy where every swipe lands; they taunt you all the way through. Three days for **five towers**, each a different job, with the allies your earlier choices earned (the Bathyr, your crew or the Saltwrit, the terrace folk, Varro's archers, a sellsword). On the Gnomon, Corvane in a Fury bout that remembers your habits, then **the Reaping**: a souls meter that fills every round and with his own men, spent at six on one blow, with **visions of every soul it spends** first, and every Reaping that lands costs the east a league. Speak the names of the dead, retie them with a count-rope, ring them home with the Bathyr's note, smash his lantern, or brace; he'll reach for Vaelric, too. Then the counter-peal (ring the Leaning backwards), how much of the east survives, and three choices: break the great bell, lean it onto the Hinge, or keep it; free the dead or keep the lantern; wake Ysolt or let her dream. The reward is paid out of your effort: gold and the name **Hingebreaker** for everyone, **the Wrathbrand** (Corvane's blade, Unleash once a fight) for a strong showing, and more for a near-perfect one. After it, **the Visceral Realm**: foes have 15% more HP and +1 to hit and damage, Dread comes faster, Hinge remnants hold the roads back, and the roads back pay a quarter more gold with better weapon drops. Its own ambience and sounds (Fury strokes and blows, the war-drum, the Reaping's choir and boom, the Gnomon's bells), the journal's list of the Reaped, and fourteen pursuits in a new set.
- **More puzzles**: the pin-rail, the weighing, Bones, the Teeth, the shares, the skimmer, the Saltwrit signal (deduce their flag order from two hoists you've seen), and the count-rope (knot memory).
- **Experience and levels**: an XP bar on the sheet. You start at level 3 and can reach **level 50** (it was 40 before The Black Tithe; everything that wakes at 40 still wakes at 40). Fights you come through, parts and chapters you finish, and checks you pass all give XP. Each level is +2 max HP; every 9 levels +1 to hit, every 3 +1 damage, every 12 +1 AC. **Foes are scaled to your level** (+3.5% HP a level, +1 to hit every 6 levels, +1 damage every 4; the foe meter shows its level). The curve was balanced on 2026-11-04 with a fight simulator against the playtest bot's levels: an ordinary fight costs about a fifth of your HP over 3 to 4 rounds at any level, and the last bosses take 7 to 9 rounds. Old saves are credited the XP for the parts they've reached. New fight engines get scaling for free through `foeStart`; pass `{ scale: false }` for a meter that mirrors its own HP.
- **Adaptive Reasoning** (learned at level 5): every blow that lands on you teaches you the next, +1 AC against that foe (up to +2) for the fight; and every fight you come through against a kind of foe is a read on it (1 read +1 to hit, 3 reads +1 AC, 5 reads +2 to hit).
- **Mimic** (learned at level 8): the catalyst is **Mockwax**, the grey wax tallow-moths leave in the labyrinth's walls, which remembers the last shape pressed into it. Through quick hands, press it into a fresh wound to copy the blow that made it: your next hit deals that blow too, with its rider (poison and rot sicken, fire and light dazzle). Copied blows are kept as prints (3 at most), and once a fight you can lay an old print on your blade for free.
- **The roads back**: after the story so far, walk any cleared region and fight what lives there for XP and gold. At level 40, **the Cascades** wake (the realm's nightmares falling upward toward you, now that you're the heaviest thing in it): every creature becomes a **Nightmare**, nine hidden things wash loose in the old places (the Seedcrown, the Glassfang, Duskwane's Lens, the Drowned King's Gauntlet, the Bell-Iron Buckle, the Clear Verdict, Ninefold's Blade-Shell, a Splinter of the Kept Noon and the Kinstone Ring), and the Cascades lead to **the Underdream**, a dream that learned to fight like you. Beat it and you choose whether the Cascades keep running.
- **Gangs of the Blood-bought Roads**: bandit gangs work the roads between the kingdoms. You meet them on the road between chapters (45% at each chapter's start), on the roads back's **Take the Blood-bought Roads** (pick two neighbouring kingdoms you've cleared), and now and then while you walk the old roads. There are **50 named gangs**, each with its own painted mark (an inline SVG emblem built from a shape, a division, two colours and a charge), a motto, and one dirty trick (ambush, smoke-pots, the net, barbed steel, blood-oath, light fingers, shield wall, old hexes, fire-arrows). Every gang is four: a **Hexer** (mage: heals the others, jinxes and curses you), a **Shot** (ranged: an aimed shot every third time), a **Wall** (tank: +2 AC on anyone else while it stands) and a **Knife** (DPS). One health bar, but you pick who to go for, and your blows carry over when your target drops. Every meeting rolls a **haul** (twelve kinds) and a weapon of the gang's own, named for them. Pay the toll, slip past, fight, or (at Infamy 3) **call them out by name**. Afterwards tie them up for the watch (bounty, lean **Roadwarden**), let them go for a cut (they may come back with a grudge, lean **Road-king**), or take their colours (+1 Infamy). Your **road name** climbs with Infamy, from Unknown on the roads to Terror of the Blood-bought Roads. **Contender**: every fight you win counts; six ranks (1, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200 wins) take a twentieth off in towns each, pay a purse for your stories in every town, and give free rooms (III), free honing (V) and a healer's gift (VI). After twelve broken gangs, the legendary **High Weave** sends for you: climb the Needle, pick the right line across, and fight Ysolde Marrowlace's four on swaying spider-silk (it drops a level at half health). The prize is **Threadfang**, a Heartfire sword with the **Spider's Line** active (two blows can't reach you, then you drop for +2d6 with advantage), advantage and +2 on every climbing check in the game, and the rooftops in every town. The journal keeps a gang ledger of all fifty marks, and a **What's new** page with the update log.
- **The Blood-bought Roads**: the roads between the kingdoms, cut by convicts in chains, with somebody under every milestone. Eleven named stretches, each with its own nature: the Blood-Bought Way, the Red-Coin Road, the Widow's Tradeway, the Ransom Run, the Blood-Bought Pike, the Sellsword's Track, the Blood-Bought Mile, the Blood-Bought Run, the Gallows March, the Salt-Toll Stair and the Wreckers' Shore. Monster country (help a caravan with a body count), sellsword roads (escort for half the profits, and duel or stare down a rival company's captain), conquered land (locals ambushing to take it back: hear them out, pay the land-price, or force it), blood-tolls (a gang on the bridge: coin or blood, duel the Wall for the toll-box, or cut the ropes), plus gang wars, wagon chases, and the rare Red-Coin Peddler. Seven pursuits in the set The Blood-bought Roads.
- **Bleeding**: a slashing or piercing hit opens a bleeding wound 1 time in 4 (always on a natural 20; 1 in 2 from bleeders like the Glass-cutter, Scree-lurker, Shade-shearer, Kite-eel and Veil). Each wound bleeds at the start of every round: 1d4, or 1d6 from a heavy weapon (d8 or bigger) once you're level 9, plus 1 every 9 levels; up to 3 wounds. It shows under your HP and can't take your last HP by itself. A **bandage** stops it: free with quick hands (a fumble only closes the worst wound), or *Bandage the bleeding* as your move; Field Mending's binding stops it too. Still bleeding when the fight ends? Bandage it, or tie it off and bleed one more round. Bandages (the old field dressings) come 2 with your first wound, get rolled from spare cloth scraps between parts (up to 3), and can be bought in town markets (2 for 5 gold) or rolled at the bench. Feat: *Pressure, Then the Knot* (stop bleeding 10 times).
- **Nightmare fights**: a road Nightmare brings **Dread** (0 to 6, under its health bar). Its hits, its stare and its turning raise it; at 2 your combo caps at +1, at 4 your hands shake (quick hands and dig-in get harder), and at 6 you're **Overtaken**: you lose the fight, its spoils and half the gold you carry (banked gold is safe), and it waits on that region's roads with the HP it had left. Rootbreakers, perfect quick hands and digging in push Dread back. At 4 Dread Burr freezes until you spend a turn calling him back, and Tyra (bond 2+) steadies you once a fight before you're Overtaken. Each Nightmare hangs from 1 to 3 **Cascade threads**: *Cut a thread* is an attack at −2 that does no damage but strips part of its +2/+2 Nightmare bonus and its Dread, and cutting them all doubles its weapon drop roll. At half health every Nightmare turns into its **feared shape**, another creature of the realm, and keeps both tricks (Adaptive Reasoning shows the fear in advance; the bestiary records it once seen).
- **The Deafstones**: 30 lore tablets, three in every region, carved in stone, lead and gold by the people the old wardens (the Hushkeepers) made forget, since a whisper can't reach something that can't hear. Two in each region wait in the story; the third, and any you missed, wait on the roads back. Each one is a charcoal **rubbing** mini-game (rub every square, steer round the cracks; smudges cost words), some are in scripts you learn later (Moored knot-cords, Hesk song-script, Lidless shadow-script, Bathyr lead) and fill in once you can read them, and together each region's three tell its **founding story**. Finding all three gives +1 on checks there that aren't attacks; the map gets a carved mark; all 30 open Ilka's Stone. Leave each stone standing or pry it up for the coins underneath: a quiet **Hear or Hush** balance, read out in words in the journal, keeps count of that and of the big choices of the story.
- **The calendar**: time as atmosphere, not a timer. Twelve months of twelve days (Sapwake, Mudtide, Greenshoot in spring; Tallsun, Hayfire, Longlight in summer; Gleaning, Leafdrop, Smokewind in autumn; Rimewane, Ironfrost, Candlewait in winter) and a six-day week that ends on market day. Every part moves the clock a watch (dawn, day, dusk, night), the road between kingdoms takes days, a rest runs on to morning, and every errand on the roads back takes a watch or two. The date shows under your purse and on its own journal page, per save slot (old saves get a date about where a new journey would be). At **night** the gangs on the Blood-bought Roads ambush you (spot them in the dark, or they shoot first; the take is a quarter fatter), and you can make camp to wait for dark or for morning. **Market day** takes a fifth off town stalls. Four **festivals**: the Root-Waking (a gift), the Night of Lamps (no night ambushes, a free round), the Fair of Full Barns (half price, a sack-race) and the Long Candle (free rooms, a night's watch). **Winter** tolls cost half as much again. No deadlines. Five pursuits in the set The Turning Year.
- **The Commoner's Almanac** (seasonal folklore, all atmosphere): the roads and towns comment on the season (mud and full ditches in spring, tariff rows and sleeping guards in summer, leaf-drifted shrines and orchard lights in autumn, frozen wells and lamp-oil stockpiles in winter), festival oddities, and a one-line note when the month turns. Every month the bar has a treasure rumour that you can follow up for a watch; some are true (gold) and some are nonsense. Five named byways on the Blood-bought Roads (the Beggar's Mile, King's Regret, Crooked Mercy Road, the Long Apology, Halfpenny Hill) and everybody's own explanation of the roads' name. The Almanac (3 gold at any market) gives a proverb, a prediction, a joke and gossip for each month. Four things happen once a year in towns: the wandering storyteller (Night of Lamps), the Golden Pig race (3rd–4th Hayfire), the merchant of useless luxuries (7th–8th Smokewind) and the annual argument (2nd–3rd Ironfrost), which resumes where it left off. Four pursuits in the set The Commoner's Almanac.
- **Blade to Blade (duels)**: some fights are duels, fought on a stroke pad. Each exchange has three phases: **Read** (the foe's blade streaks across the pad), **Answer** (draw the stroke that meets it, the opposite way; quick and exact is a riposte, one direction off catches half) and **Press** (up to three strokes of your own). Strokes are four shapes in eight directions (a line cuts, a curve feints and pulls the guard, a tap thrusts and always lands, a loop binds the blade), so chains of up to three give 6,174 attacks. The foe guards two of four edges; every stroke builds Pressure, and a full bar breaks the guard for a double blow. Repeat a stroke and they guard it; meet them again and they remember your favourite. Twelve named **Forms** (kept in the journal's Form Book across every journey), a **salle** in every town on the roads back (journeyman, provost and master, blunted blades, three schools to take up), duels with **rival captains** when you board on the Open Reach, and single combat with a gang's **Knife**. Mouse and keyboard (QWE/AD/ZXC or arrows, Shift to feint, S thrust, R bind, Enter ends the press), a button pad, and a **Steady** switch with no timer. Locked in, Menace (more time to answer) and Blinded (parry by the whistle of the blade) carry over. Eight pursuits in the set Blade to Blade.
- **Tactical Advantage (battle style)**: chosen once per save slot, and changeable in the journal: Classic, Tactical Advantage, or ask each fight. With Tactical Advantage, company fights on the roads back (caravans, the salt-tax wagon, road-raiders) and boardings (the Open Reach and the Black Tithe) are fought on the region's battle map with your whole company as units: you, Burr, Tyra, your sellswords and, on a deck, up to two sworn crew. Turn order runs on speed; each unit moves and plays one card a turn, then chooses a facing (+2 to hit from the side, +4 from behind). The map's ground rules all apply. ◆ Resonance (+1 a turn, 3 at most) pays for Heavy Blow, Shove and the companions' specials; pirate tricks are cards, once a battle. Drop every captain and the rest strike; five of theirs fit on the field, the rest step up as others fall; a high Plunder makes guards throw down their arms. "Let them fight it out" plays it for you. Wins count in the journal across slots: 12 earns **Tactical Warrior** and the **Marshal's Compass** (set your people before each grid battle and open with more ◆; in other fights, advantage on your first swing). The engine is `tacCompany` in `index.html`; `companyOr(map, o)` sends a company fight to it when the slot's style asks for it.
- **Progress saving** in your browser, so you can continue from the last part you reached.
- **A journal** of each journey, kept in your browser: time played, deaths, close calls (when the axe held you at 1 HP), fights, gold found and spent, every item found, bounties claimed and treasure dug up. Each chapter ends with a summary card, and the journal (the *Journal* button in your items) keeps a table of finished chapters, totals across every journey, and your earlier runs.
- **The story so far.** Continuing a save opens with a short recap of that run: the choices you made in each chapter, who is with you, where you stopped and what you carry. Skip it, turn it off, or read it again any time at the top of the journal. Each chapter's lines come from the `RECAP` table in `index.html`, keyed by the chapter's slug in `CHAPTERS`; a new chapter should add an entry there.
- **A map that grows.** The *Map* button (next to *Journal*) opens a hand-inked map of the realm. Each chapter you reach lifts the fog off its region, with a mark for where you are now; the western sea stays uncharted. Each save slot keeps its own map. Regions come from the `MAP` table in `index.html`, keyed by the chapter's slug in `CHAPTERS`, so a new chapter adds its region with one entry (its place, glyphs from `MAP_ART`, labels, road and the fog it clears) and no redraw.

## Status

**Main-hand weapons.** Besides Vaelric's Axe, you can buy the Hollowpine Spear, the Cairnstone Maul, the Wasp-Needle Rapier and the Cinderbrand from the travelling armourer Hobb Tallis at the start of each chapter, or from Mattie Gorse in Rahdaria. The Thornwood Longbow (40 gold) carries **Onslaught**: once a fight, three arrows at once, each finding its own target (up to three foes in a group fight, or 3d6 on a single foe). Bows also drop in the field. Swap between them any time from the Bag. In Rahdaria, solving a case, closing an inquiry, breaking the blight, beating a General or winning the siege earns you **three cheers** from the city.

**Field weapons.** Won fights can drop weapons in five qualities of our own: Ashen (smoke grey, 12%), Tempered (bronze, 8%), Rootbound (verdigris, 4%), Gloaming (lavender, 1.5%) and Heartfire (ember gold, 0.34%). Heartfire has a safety net: each won fight without one adds 0.05%, and the 200th dry fight always drops one. Better weapons carry traits (Reach, Searing, Crushing, Keen, Thirsting) and a once-a-fight active fired with quick hands (Phase Strike, Become Ethereal, Heartflare, Sap Mend, Gloam Veil). The Curator's vault always holds the **Spectral Dagger of Rahdaria** (1d6 + 4, +2 to hit, Phase Strike for 34, Become Ethereal). You carry six found weapons; past that, the worst is sold.

**The weapon harness.** Four weapons ride ready (five at level 20, six at level 40), plus the axe. In a fight, Swap weapon to one is free once a turn, a second swap gives the foe advantage on its next attack, and a weapon from the pack costs your next swing.

**Towns and sanctuaries on the roads back.** After the story so far, every cleared region keeps its towns and quiet places, and you can visit any of them from the roads back: inns, markets (potions, elixirs, salvage), smiths (weapons and honing), banks in Ashgate and Rahdaria, the Library of Roots and the Vitreous Library (learn a skill for 30 gold), Sister Wren's Field Mending at Vellum Rest, bars for rumours, and sanctuaries (the Glade, Vellum Rest, the Temple of the Kept Noon, the hanging gardens, the Library of Roots) where you rest for free and the road's sicknesses are eased.

**The Cairnwright's Wages, a treasure hunt on the roads back.** Nan Ferrier keeps a list of the three hundred navvies who laid Empress Adrianna's roads under Merrit Sallow, the Cairnwright, and were never paid. Sallow hid their wages and the Empress's champion's chest, and cut the way to them into seven milestones, each a different puzzle: a four-dial road cipher (Mile Nought, the Library of Roots), culvert stones to turn until the sand drains (the Burning Sands), a toll-scale with one false weight and two weighings (the Hushfen), three foremen's statues where only one tells the truth (Carrowmere), a lens collar of sun-signs to crack by lit and glimmer (Sulmara), a floor of mile-stones to walk to a sung total (Mount Corrach), and a counter-chain to balance with kinstones (the Undermoon). Every stone gives a random mile-mark, and the Toll-House lock at Rahdaria's land gate wants all seven, in the order its riddle names the stones. A rival, Ambrose Penhallow, is on the same trail. Any puzzle can be forced with a check (and maybe a fight), so nobody gets stuck. The cache holds **Empress Adrianna's Champion Set** (+2 AC, +8 max HP, +1 to hit, +10% Evade, +1 damage), 12 healing vials, 3 bags of caltrops (now usable in any fight with quick hands), 2 medium traps and 500 gold, with three cheers, plus the navvies' wages: pay them (inns on the roads back become free) or keep them. The ledger is in your items and journal, and the map marks every stone you read. Eight new pursuits in set 12.

**The Warden's Weapon Chest.** At level 40, Hobb Tallis or Mattie Gorse sells it for 1,000 gold. Six places, kept apart from every save, so it's shared by all three slots and nothing that happens to a run (death, a replay from your last rest, Rahdaria Falls) touches it.

**First-time tips.** The first time a system turns up (fights and combos, fight moves, bleeding, the axe holding you at 1 HP, quick hands, weapon swaps, digging in, spoils, levels, your company, weather, road creatures, company battles, volleys and cover, mines and caltrops, terrain hazards, the trapper's kit, the Deafstones), a short note explains it. Tips are remembered per browser across all three slots, and the journal's **Field guide** lists every one you've seen, with a switch to stop new ones.

**Mines in a fight.** You can set two mines in any one fight (rune mines and regional mines together), and caltrops once. When a captain goes down in a company battle, one or two soldiers run.

Parts I to LXXVI are complete. The story ends on the shingle at Stillwater, the island where the Hushkeepers began, with the Tithe Fleet risen under you, hanged, or gone home remembering, and twenty-nine of the Hushed watching you from the wall. Part LXXVII is still to come.
